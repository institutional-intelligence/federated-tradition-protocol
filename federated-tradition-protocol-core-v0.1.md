# Federated Tradition Protocol
## Religion-Neutral Core v0.1

**Status:** Draft  
**Version:** 0.1

The **Federated Tradition Protocol (FTP)** is an open framework for designing durable, federated traditions and institutions.

FTP does not define what a tradition must believe, teach, celebrate, symbolize, remember, or regard as sacred. It defines institutional structures through which a tradition can organize itself, preserve continuity, distribute authority, resolve conflict, reproduce local communities, and adapt across time and place.

An FTP implementation may be religious, philosophical, cultural, artistic, civic, or otherwise.

The governing principle of the Core is:

> **Structure is specified. Content is local.**

---

# 1. Purpose

Ideas can spread without institutions.

Institutions allow ideas, practices, relationships, records, and communities to persist beyond particular founders, leaders, personalities, and generations.

FTP therefore treats tradition-building as an institutional design problem.

The Core provides a minimum architecture for communities that wish to remain locally adaptable while maintaining enough common structure to recognize one another, cooperate, reproduce themselves, and federate.

FTP does not require centralized control, a headquarters, a single founder, a canonical theology, a universal ritual system, or uniform cultural content.

---

# 2. The Specification Gradient

FTP distinguishes between **institutional structure** and **traditional content**.

An implementation should specify most strongly those matters necessary for institutional continuity and accountability, while leaving substantive content to the communities using the protocol.

As a general rule:

- **Governance:** strongly specified
- **Accountability and process:** strongly specified
- **Institutional continuity:** strongly specified
- **Ethics and conduct:** implementation-defined, but explicit
- **Practice and ceremony:** locally adaptable
- **Narrative, symbolism, philosophy, or theology:** implementation-defined
- **Metaphysical belief:** outside the FTP Core

FTP therefore standardizes the machinery of institutional durability more strongly than the content carried by that machinery.

---

# 3. Institutional Layers

An FTP-compatible system may contain three institutional layers:

## 3.1 Core

The **Core** defines the minimum institutional requirements necessary for FTP compatibility.

The Core is religion-neutral and content-neutral.

## 3.2 Implementation

An **Implementation** applies the Core to a particular tradition, philosophy, culture, project, or community.

An Implementation may define:

- principles or teachings
- ethical commitments
- symbols and narratives
- rituals or ceremonies
- calendars and observances
- membership expectations
- specialized offices
- cultural or regional adaptations
- additional compatibility requirements

An Implementation may add requirements beyond the Core but may not remove Core requirements while representing itself as FTP-compatible.

## 3.3 Local Community

A **Local Community** is a self-governing body operating under an Implementation.

Implementations may use other names for this unit, including chapter, assembly, lodge, circle, congregation, hearth, society, guild, community, or locally appropriate terminology.

Local Communities retain authority over matters delegated to local discretion by their Implementation.

---

# 4. Core Institutional Primitives

An FTP-compatible Implementation must provide functional equivalents of the following primitives.

## P1 — Local Autonomy

The Local Community is the basic self-governing unit of the system.

Authority not explicitly assigned to a federation-level or implementation-level institution remains local.

Local Communities may adapt practices, terminology, schedules, ceremonies, and other locally determined content without seeking permission from a central authority, provided they remain within the requirements of their Implementation and this Core.

---

## P2 — Explicit Governance

Each Local Community must maintain an identifiable governance process.

That process must specify at minimum:

- who participates in institutional decisions;
- how decisions are made;
- how officers or equivalent function-holders are selected;
- how finances and shared property are governed;
- how governing rules may be amended; and
- how institutional decisions are recorded.

No FTP-compatible community may rely exclusively on informal personal authority for governance.

---

## P3 — Functional Authority

Institutional authority attaches to **functions and offices**, not inherently to persons.

An office exists because the institution requires a function to be performed.

Every office must therefore have:

- a defined scope;
- identifiable duties;
- identifiable limits;
- a method of selection or appointment;
- a method of accountability; and
- a method of succession, replacement, or removal.

Holding one office does not automatically confer authority outside the function of that office.

---

## P4 — Authorized Authority

No claim of personal status, insight, charisma, authorship, revelation, lineage, expertise, celebrity, seniority, founding role, or other personal distinction independently confers institutional authority.

Institutional authority exists only where an acknowledged institutional process grants it.

A founder may found an institution.

An expert may advise an institution.

A teacher may teach.

A ceremonial leader may lead ceremonies.

A charismatic person may influence others.

None of these facts, by themselves, confer unrestricted governing authority.

---

## P5 — Accountability

Every person exercising institutional authority must remain accountable to a defined process outside that person's unilateral control.

An FTP-compatible Implementation must provide mechanisms through which:

- decisions can be reviewed;
- finances and shared resources can be accounted for;
- officers can be questioned;
- misconduct can be addressed;
- officers can be removed or replaced; and
- institutional rules apply to founders and leaders as well as ordinary members.

No office may be permanently immune from review.

---

## P6 — Institutional Memory

A durable institution must preserve information beyond the memory of its current participants.

Each Local Community must maintain records sufficient to reconstruct its institutional history and current operation.

These should include, where applicable:

- governing rules;
- amendments;
- significant decisions;
- officer succession;
- financial records;
- membership or participation records appropriate to the Implementation;
- major disputes and their institutional outcomes; and
- historical or cultural materials the community considers necessary for continuity.

An Implementation may define additional archives, chronicles, registers, canons, libraries, or records appropriate to its tradition.

Institutional memory should survive individual officers.

---

## P7 — Right of Exit

Participation in an FTP-compatible institution is voluntary.

Individuals must be able to leave a Local Community.

Local Communities must be able to leave a federation or Implementation-level affiliation.

Exit may end access to offices, institutional property, names, credentials, or other resources belonging to the institution, but exit itself must not be prevented through coercion, threats, or institutional retaliation.

Federation is therefore based on continuing consent rather than permanent submission.

---

## P8 — Due Process

No member, officer, or Local Community may lose institutional standing through an undefined or purely arbitrary process.

An FTP-compatible Implementation must establish procedures appropriate to its scale for:

1. notice of an alleged violation or dispute;
2. opportunity to respond;
3. review by a person or body with defined authority;
4. a decision based on stated institutional rules;
5. proportional consequences; and
6. some mechanism for reconsideration or appeal where serious sanctions are imposed.

Emergency measures may be used where immediate safety or preservation of institutional property reasonably requires them, but emergency measures must be temporary and subject to subsequent review.

---

## P9 — Conflict Resolution

Conflict is treated as a normal institutional condition rather than evidence that an institution has failed.

An FTP-compatible Implementation must provide a defined pathway for resolving disputes.

A recommended progression is:

**informal resolution → mediation → institutional review → appeal → separation**

Not every dispute requires every stage, and serious safety or misconduct concerns may require immediate formal review.

The essential requirement is that conflict be processed through known procedures rather than determined solely by seniority, popularity, personal influence, or unilateral authority.

---

## P10 — Succession and Continuity

No essential institutional function should depend permanently upon one irreplaceable person.

Every required office, account, archive, credential, record, and institutional resource must be transferable to an authorized successor.

Implementations and Local Communities should maintain procedures for:

- expiration or renewal of terms;
- resignation;
- incapacity;
- removal;
- vacancies;
- emergency replacement; and
- orderly transfer of records, property, accounts, credentials, and responsibilities.

A tradition that cannot survive the departure of its founder has not yet achieved institutional durability.

---

# 5. Federation

FTP uses **federation** to combine local autonomy with shared compatibility.

A federation is an association of Local Communities and/or Implementations that voluntarily recognize common standards while retaining substantial self-government.

A federation must specify:

- the requirements for affiliation;
- what authority, if any, exists above the Local Community;
- what matters remain exclusively local;
- how shared decisions are made;
- how new communities are recognized;
- how disputes between communities are resolved;
- how affiliation may be revoked; and
- how a community may voluntarily disaffiliate.

Federation-level authority should be limited to matters that require interoperability, shared identity, common resources, or enforcement of explicitly shared standards.

Federation does not imply unlimited hierarchy.

---

# 6. Minimum Compatibility

An Implementation may describe itself as **FTP-compatible** when it:

1. identifies itself as an Implementation of the Federated Tradition Protocol;
2. preserves the ten Core Institutional Primitives in functional form;
3. publishes or otherwise makes available its governing structure;
4. distinguishes institutional authority from merely personal authority;
5. provides a defined governance and accountability process;
6. preserves a meaningful Right of Exit;
7. provides due process and conflict-resolution procedures;
8. provides for institutional memory and succession;
9. defines the relationship between Local Communities and any federation-level authority; and
10. clearly identifies which matters are shared requirements and which are locally adaptable.

Functional equivalence matters more than terminology.

An Implementation need not use the words *chapter*, *assembly*, *officer*, *federation*, or *tradition* if its own terminology performs the same institutional functions.

---

# 7. Local Adaptation

FTP assumes that durable traditions must be capable of adaptation.

Implementations may therefore create regional, cultural, linguistic, philosophical, ritual, artistic, or other distributions of themselves.

Local adaptation may modify content without breaking compatibility where the underlying institutional requirements remain intact.

This produces a general rule:

> **Preserve the interface; adapt the implementation.**

Two communities may therefore differ substantially in language, symbolism, practice, calendar, aesthetics, philosophy, or worldview while remaining institutionally interoperable.

---

# 8. Forkability

FTP is designed to be forked.

A person or community may use the Core as the starting architecture for a new Implementation.

An Implementation may itself be forked into new Implementations.

A Local Community may adapt locally within the permissions of its Implementation or separate and continue independently under another structure.

The purpose of the protocol is not to preserve control by the original author.

The purpose is to make institutional architecture reusable.

---

# 9. What the Core Does Not Specify

The FTP Core deliberately takes no position on:

- theology;
- metaphysics;
- supernatural claims;
- gods or absence of gods;
- scriptures or canons;
- mythology;
- political ideology;
- cultural identity;
- aesthetics;
- ritual language;
- calendars;
- dietary practices;
- sexual ethics;
- family structure;
- cosmology;
- sacred geography;
- philosophical doctrine; or
- ultimate meaning.

An Implementation may address any or all of these.

Their absence from the Core is intentional.

FTP specifies institutional architecture, not substantive worldview.

---

# 10. Reference Implementations

Reference Implementations demonstrate how the religion-neutral Core can be instantiated in concrete traditions.

The first planned reference implementation is the **European Federated Tradition (EFT)**.

EFT applies FTP architecture to a modern federated religious and cultural tradition drawing upon institutional patterns found across pre-Christian European traditions.

EFT contains substantive elements — including household continuity, ancestral remembrance, local stewardship, seasonal observance, reciprocity, hospitality, ritual structure, and sacred plurality — that belong to that Implementation rather than to the religion-neutral FTP Core.

Future Implementations need not share those elements.

---

# 11. Versioning and Development

This document is **Version 0.1**.

It is an early specification intended for experimentation, implementation, criticism, and revision.

Future versions may refine terminology, compatibility requirements, federation mechanics, implementation templates, versioning rules, and interoperability standards.

Changes to the Core should favor institutional durability without unnecessarily constraining the substantive content of Implementations.

---

# 12. Core Design Principle

The Federated Tradition Protocol exists to make a particular transformation possible:

**idea → practice → community → institution → continuity**

Its central design principle is:

> **Specify enough structure for the institution to survive.  
> Leave enough freedom for the tradition to live.**
