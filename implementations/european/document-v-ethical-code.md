# Document V — The Ethical Code

## Standards of Reciprocity, Hospitality, Reputation, Consent, and Stewardship

**Version 0.1 — Draft**

**Subordinate to Document I — The Charter and Document II — The Federation Protocol.**

This Document translates the ethical Foundational Principles — Reciprocity,
Hospitality, and Personal Reputation — into standards a Chapter can actually apply
to real conduct, and adds two standards implied throughout the Federation’s other
documents but not yet stated on their own terms: Consent and Stewardship.

## Preamble

The Federation does not require its members to hold a shared metaphysics. It does
require them to hold a shared ethic — not because ethics is easier to agree on than
theology, but because ethics is what actually determines whether a community is
safe, trustworthy, and worth belonging to.

This Code is deliberately built around conduct and its consequences, not around
abstract virtue language. Its central claim is stated plainly:

> **No one is owed trust, office, or standing by virtue of who they claim to be, what
> they claim to have experienced, or what they claim to believe. Trust, office, and
> standing are earned by what a person actually does, over time, and can be lost the
> same way.**

This Code does not require supernatural sanction to function. A community that
keeps it will function whether or not any of its members believe the gods are
watching.

## Article I — Purpose and Application

**Section 1.1.** This Document elaborates Document II, Articles VI (Reciprocity),
VII (Hospitality), and VIII (Personal Reputation), and establishes two additional
standards — Consent (Article IV) and Stewardship (Article V) — that are presupposed
elsewhere in the Federation’s documents but not separately defined until now.

**Section 1.2.** Violations of this Code are addressed through Document VII
(Dispute Procedure) and, at the Chapter level, Document III, Article VI. This
Document defines the standard of conduct; it does not itself define the disciplinary
process.

**Section 1.3.** This Code applies to conduct within Chapter life — gatherings,
offices, Chapter-affiliated spaces and communications — and, where conduct outside
those settings materially affects the safety or trust of the Chapter, to that conduct as
well.

## Article II — Reciprocity

**Section 2.1. Standard.** A member who receives a benefit from the Chapter, the
Federation, another member, or the land the Chapter inhabits, incurs a reasonable
obligation of return. “Reasonable” is measured by what was received, not by an
unlimited or open-ended debt.

**Section 2.2. Application.** This standard governs, among other things:

- **Use of Chapter resources, space, or labor** — a member who repeatedly draws
  on Chapter resources without ever contributing time, labor, offering, or material
  support is in tension with this Article, regardless of stated intentions.
- **Mentorship and teaching** — a member taught a skill, rite, or body of knowledge
  by another incurs some reasonable obligation to pass it on, support the teacher, or
  otherwise sustain the relationship.
- **The land** — a Chapter’s use of a place for gathering or ritual (Document IV)
  creates an obligation of stewardship toward that place (Article V, below).

**Section 2.3. Limit.** Reciprocity does not require equal or immediate return, and
may be discharged in a different form than it was received (labor for teaching,
offering for hospitality, future service for present help). It is violated only by a
sustained, one-directional pattern of extraction, not by any single instance of
receiving more than one gives.

## Article III — Hospitality

**Section 3.1. Standard.** A host and a guest hold mutual, bounded duties toward
one another, as summarized in Document II, Article VII.

**Section 3.2. Host Duties.** A host (a Chapter, officer, or member hosting a
gathering) owes a guest: reasonable physical and emotional safety, clarity about what
the gathering involves and what is expected of attendees, and basic courtesy
regardless of the guest’s beliefs or background.

**Section 3.3. Guest Duties.** A guest owes a host: respect for the host’s property
and space, honesty about their intentions in attending, compliance with reasonable
norms stated in advance, and, where the guest becomes a member, eventual
reciprocity under Article II.

**Section 3.4. Explicit Non-Obligation.** Hospitality under this Article does not
obligate a host to admit, tolerate, or continue hosting a guest who violates Chapter
norms, misrepresents their intentions, or poses a safety concern. Hospitality is not a
right of unconditional or indefinite access.

## Article IV — Consent

**Section 4.1. Standard.** No rite, offering, teaching, physical contact, or disclosure
of personal information may be required of a member or guest without their informed
agreement. Participation in the Federation’s spiritual life is never coerced, and no
ritual or social pressure may be used to override a stated refusal.

**Section 4.2. Application to Ritual Office.** No Ritual Office-holder, teacher, or
officer may use their role, claimed insight, or position of trust to obtain participation,
disclosure, contact, or compliance that would not otherwise be freely given. A pattern
of using ritual or teaching authority to pressure members — sexually, financially, or
otherwise — is a severe violation of this Code and grounds for immediate removal
from office under Document III, Article VI, regardless of the vote threshold ordinarily
required for lesser matters.

**Section 4.3. Minors.** Where a Chapter includes minors in household or
coming-of-age contexts (Document IV, Article II), consent standards for adult
members apply with heightened protection, and any physical, ritual, or teaching
contact with a minor must additionally comply with the informed involvement of the
minor’s parent or guardian and with applicable law.

## Article V — Stewardship

**Section 5.1. Standard.** A Chapter and its members owe an obligation of care
toward the specific places, ecosystems, and material resources they use — extending
Local Stewardship (Document II, Article V) from a ritual practice into a general
ethical standard.

**Section 5.2. Application.** This standard governs use of shared or borrowed
gathering spaces (leave them as found or better), the ecological features a Chapter
has adopted a relationship with (Document II, Section 5.1), and the Chapter’s own
property and finances (Document III, Article IV) — all held for the benefit of the
whole and future members, not consumed by the present membership alone.

**Section 5.3. Extension to Future Generations.** Consistent with Household
Continuity (Document II, Article III) and Ancestral Remembrance (Document II,
Article IV), a Chapter’s use of its resources should reasonably account for the
members and Chapters that will come after it, not only its present convenience.

## Article VI — Reputation

**Section 6.1. Standard.** Reputation within the Federation is built and lost through
conduct, observed over time, not through claimed status, lineage, or spiritual
experience (Document II, Article VIII; Charter Article VII).

**Section 6.2. Non-Exhaustive Indicators of Good Standing:**

- keeping stated commitments;
- competence in one’s chosen role or office;
- honesty, including about one’s own limitations or mistakes;
- generosity proportionate to one’s means;
- willingness to carry an equitable share of Chapter labor;
- accepting responsibility for harm caused, without requiring it be extracted through
  conflict;
- defending vulnerable members when reasonably able to do so.

**Section 6.3. Non-Exhaustive Indicators of Poor Standing:**

- a sustained pattern of receiving Chapter benefit without contribution (Article II);
- betrayal of confidence or trust reasonably placed;
- chronic unreliability inconsistent with one’s stated commitments;
- dishonesty about material facts affecting other members;
- exploiting hospitality, teaching relationships, or ritual office for personal
  advantage (Article III, Article IV);
- coercive or abusive conduct toward another member.

**Section 6.4. Application.** Reputation concerns under this Article are ordinarily
addressed informally in the first instance (Document III, Article VI, stage 1) and
escalate only if the underlying conduct is serious or persistent. This Code is not a
basis for social exclusion over disagreement, unpopularity, or theological difference
protected under Document II, Article II.

## Article VII — Relationship to Discipline

**Section 7.1.** A credible, serious allegation under this Code — particularly under
Article IV (Consent) — should be treated by Chapter officers as at least as significant
as a financial or safety matter, and is not diminished by the accused party’s ritual
standing, founding role, or claimed spiritual authority (Charter Article VII; Document
III, Section 3.5).

**Section 7.2.** Nothing in this Code requires a Chapter to tolerate an ongoing safety
risk while a full Document VII process unfolds; Document III, Section 6.2’s
emergency interim suspension applies in full to violations of this Code.
