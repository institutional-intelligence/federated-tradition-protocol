# Document VII — The Dispute Procedure

## Resolution of Conflict at the Chapter and Federation Level

**Version 0.1 — Draft**

**Subordinate to Document I — The Charter.**

This Document is the final piece of the founding document set, and formalizes the
dispute process referenced throughout Documents I–VI: the five-stage process at the
Chapter level (Document III, Article VI) and the previously undefined process for
disputes and disaffiliation at the Federation level.

## Preamble

Every other document in this set assumes that conflict will happen — between
members, between an officer and the Assembly, between a Chapter and the
Federation — and that the tradition’s durability depends on having a real answer for
it, rather than trusting each dispute to be settled by whoever has the most conviction,
the most seniority, or the most claimed spiritual authority.

This Document is that answer. Its governing principle is the same one Charter
Article VII establishes for ordinary institutional authority, applied specifically to
conflict:

> **No dispute is resolved by appeal to revelation, lineage, or claimed spiritual
> insight. It is resolved by process, evidence of conduct, and, where necessary, a
> vote of the people accountable for the outcome.**

## Article I — Purpose and Scope

**Section 1.1.** This Document governs two distinct kinds of dispute:

- **Chapter-level disputes** — between members, or between a member and an
  officer, within a single Chapter (formalizing the stages already introduced in
  Document III, Article VI).
- **Federation-level disputes** — between a Chapter and the Federation, between
  two Chapters, between a Chapter and its Distribution, or concerning an alleged
  violation of the Charter, the Compatibility Blacklist, or the Foundational
  Principles.

**Section 1.2.** Where Document III’s Chapter-level bylaws and this Document
overlap, Document III governs the Chapter’s internal procedure, and this Document
governs when and how a matter may escalate beyond the Chapter.

## Article II — Principles Governing All Disputes

**Section 2.1. Due Process.** No member, officer, or Chapter may lose standing,
office, or affiliation without notice of the specific conduct or violation alleged, and a
reasonable opportunity to respond, except under the emergency provisions of
Article IX.

**Section 2.2. No Authority from Revelation.** Claimed spiritual insight, revelation,
or ancestral sanction is not admissible as grounds for any outcome under this
Document, consistent with Charter Article VII. Evidence under this Document
consists of conduct, communication, and its documented or witnessed effects.

**Section 2.3. Right of Exit Preserved.** At any stage of any dispute, a member may
exercise their Right of Exit (Charter Section 5.3; Document III, Section 1.3) rather
than continue through the process. Exercising this right ends that member’s exposure
to further process but does not, by itself, resolve harm to others or obligations already
incurred.

**Section 2.4. Proportionality.** The severity of a response should be proportionate
to the conduct at issue. Minor, first-instance, or good-faith disagreements should
ordinarily be resolved informally (Article III, Stage 1) and should not be escalated
directly to removal or disaffiliation absent genuine severity or a repeated pattern.

**Section 2.5. Safety Exception.** Nothing in this Article requires a Chapter or the
Federation to delay protective action against an ongoing safety risk; see Article IX.

## Article III — The Chapter-Level Process

**Section 3.1.** This Article restates, as binding Federation standard, the five-stage
process a Chapter must provide under Document III, Article VI:

1. **Informal Resolution** — direct conversation between the parties, or facilitated
   by a trusted member or the Mediator office (Document VI, Article V), attempted
   before any formal step is taken, except under Article IX.
2. **Mediation** — a structured, facilitative process (Document VI, Section 5.2) if
   informal resolution fails or is inappropriate to the matter.
3. **Assembly Review** — if mediation fails, is refused, or is inappropriate, the
   Assembly reviews the matter and may impose formal warning, suspension of good
   standing, or removal from membership or office, by the vote threshold set in
   Document III, Section 6.2.
4. **Appeal** — the affected party may request one Assembly reconsideration
   within the window set in Document III, Section 6.1(4).
5. **Separation** — if the matter remains unresolved, the member may exercise the
   Right of Exit, or, where the matter concerns the Chapter’s own conduct, the
   Chapter may pursue Federation-level review under Article IV of this Document.

**Section 3.2.** A Chapter may not skip stages 1–2 for a matter within its ordinary
competence to resolve informally, but may proceed directly to stage 3 where Article
IX’s safety exception applies, or where a party refuses to participate in stages 1–2 in
good faith.

## Article IV — Federation-Level Disputes

**Section 4.1. Grounds.** A matter may be brought to Federation-level review where
it involves:

- an allegation that a Chapter has violated the Compatibility Blacklist (Charter
  Article VI);
- an allegation that a Chapter has persistently failed to meet a Minimum Standard
  of Document II;
- a dispute between two Chapters, or between a Chapter and its Distribution, that
  the parties cannot resolve between themselves;
- an appeal from a Chapter-level Assembly decision, where the appealing party
  alleges the Chapter itself violated the Charter or Document II in reaching that
  decision, as distinct from an ordinary disagreement with the outcome, which is
  not independently reviewable at the Federation level.

**Section 4.2. Federation Review Body.** Pending the establishment of a standing
Federation-level Assembly or council, review under this Article shall be conducted by
a panel of delegates drawn from no fewer than three recognized Chapters or
Distributions other than those directly involved in the dispute, convened for that
purpose. Once a standing Federation body exists, it assumes this role.

**Section 4.3. Process.** Federation-level review follows the same structure as
Article III: informal resolution between the Chapters or parties involved is attempted
first; if that fails, the review panel conducts a mediation-equivalent process; if that
fails, the panel issues a determination, which may be appealed once to an enlarged
panel or standing body within a reasonable window.

**Section 4.4. Limits.** Federation-level review may not second-guess a Chapter’s
purely internal theological, ritual, or cultural choices (Document II, Articles II and
XI); it is limited to violations of the Charter, the Blacklist, and the Minimum
Standards of Document II.

## Article V — Grounds for Disaffiliation

**Section 5.1.** A Chapter’s affiliation may be revoked only for:

- a substantiated, uncured violation of the Compatibility Blacklist (Charter
  Article VI);
- persistent, uncured failure to meet the Minimum Standards of Document II,
  following notice and a reasonable cure period;
- persistent failure to maintain the governance structures required by Document III
  (Assembly, required offices, dispute process).

**Section 5.2.** Disaffiliation may never be based on a Chapter’s theological content,
ritual style, or Distribution-specific practice, provided the Minimum Standards and
Blacklist are met (Document II, Article II; Charter Article III).

## Article VI — Disaffiliation Procedure

**Section 6.1. Notice.** The Federation review body shall provide the Chapter
written notice of the specific violation alleged, with reference to the specific Article
of the Charter or Document II at issue.

**Section 6.2. Cure Period.** Except for Blacklist violations involving an ongoing
safety risk (Article IX), the Chapter shall have a reasonable cure period — ordinarily
no less than **[CURE PERIOD — e.g., 90 days]** — to remedy the violation before
further action.

**Section 6.3. Determination.** If the violation is not cured, the Federation review
body determines, by the process in Article IV, Section 4.3, whether disaffiliation is
warranted.

**Section 6.4. Appeal.** The Chapter may appeal a disaffiliation determination once,
to an enlarged panel or standing Federation body, within a reasonable window.

**Section 6.5. Effect.** Disaffiliation removes a Chapter’s standing as part of the
**European Federated Tradition (EFT)** and its right to use Federation or
Distribution names and materials as an affiliated body. It does not dissolve the
Chapter itself, which may continue independently, reconstitute under different terms,
or seek re-affiliation after demonstrated correction.

## Article VII — Voluntary Disaffiliation

**Section 7.1.** A Chapter may voluntarily disaffiliate from the Federation, or
change Distribution, by Assembly vote under Document III, Section 2.2(e), upon
reasonable notice to the Federation review body or standing Federation structure.

**Section 7.2.** Voluntary disaffiliation carries no penalty and may not be treated as
an admission of any violation.

## Article VIII — Inter-Chapter and Inter-Distribution Disputes

**Section 8.1.** Disputes between Chapters (e.g., over shared events, territory, or
naming) or between a Chapter and its Distribution (e.g., over fidelity to a Ritual
Companion, Document IV, Article VIII) are resolved under the general process of
Article IV, with particular emphasis on informal resolution given the lower stakes
typically involved.

**Section 8.2.** A Distribution has no authority to discipline or remove a Chapter’s
officers or members directly; its recourse for a Chapter’s departure from its tradition
is limited to withdrawing recognition of that Chapter as operating under its
Distribution, subject to the Chapter’s right to appeal under Article IV.

## Article IX — Emergency Measures

**Section 9.1.** Where a Chapter-level or Federation-level matter presents an
ongoing risk to a person’s safety, the emergency interim measures of Document III,
Section 6.2 (Chapter level) apply, and, at the Federation level, any three delegates
from three different recognized Chapters or Distributions may jointly impose an
interim suspension of a Chapter’s affiliation pending expedited review under Article
VI, which shall occur within **[EXPEDITED REVIEW WINDOW — e.g., 30 days]**.

**Section 9.2.** Emergency measures under this Article are explicitly interim and do
not substitute for the full process; they lapse automatically if expedited review does
not occur within the stated window.

## Article X — Confidentiality and Safety

**Section 10.1.** Proceedings under this Document, particularly those involving
Document V, Article IV (Consent) violations, should protect the reasonable privacy
of complainants and be conducted in a manner that does not expose them to
retaliation.

**Section 10.2.** Nothing in this Article may be used to shield a proceeding from the
due process protections of Section 2.1, or to prevent a party from seeking outside
legal recourse where applicable law provides it; this Document supplements, and
does not replace, any legal obligations a Chapter or its members may independently
have.

## Article XI — Records

**Section 11.1.** Outcomes of Assembly review (Article III, Stage 3) and
Federation-level determinations (Article IV) shall be recorded, consistent with
Document VI, Article IV’s Secretary duties, in a manner that preserves institutional
memory of the outcome while respecting Section 10.1’s privacy standard.

**Section 11.2.** A pattern of disputes involving the same individual or office across
multiple matters is a legitimate consideration in a later proceeding’s assessment of
severity and proportionality (Section 2.4), even where earlier matters did not
individually result in formal action.
