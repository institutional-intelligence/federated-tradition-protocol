# Document I — The Charter

## Founding Instrument of the European Federated Tradition (EFT)

**Version 0.1 — Draft**

## Preamble

We, the founding members of this tradition, establish this Charter not to reconstruct
a lost religion, but to build a living one — drawing on institutional patterns attested
across the pre-Christian traditions of Europe, adapted deliberately and openly to the
ethical standards, legal realities, and plural societies of the present day.

We make no claim to represent any single ancient people, nor to recover their beliefs
in full or in exact form. We claim only this: that certain patterns of household,
locality, reciprocity, and shared governance recur across these traditions, that those
patterns remain useful, and that we may build upon them honestly, in our own name,
for our own time.

We hold that a religious tradition is made durable not primarily by what its members
believe, but by what relationships they maintain and what institutions they build to
sustain those relationships. This Charter exists to make those institutions real,
accountable, and lasting.

## Article I — Name and Nature

**Section 1.1.** This tradition shall be known as the **European Federated Tradition**,
abbreviated **EFT**, and may be referred to informally as “the Federation.”

**Section 1.2.** The EFT is a federated religious and cultural tradition and a reference
implementation of the **Federated Tradition Protocol (FTP)**. It is not a church in the
hierarchical sense, not a single congregation, and not the property of any one founder,
teacher, or lineage. It is a federation of independent local communities (“chapters,”
“hearths,” or such other names as a chapter may adopt) that voluntarily affiliate under
a common institutional framework.

**Section 1.3.** Nothing in this Charter requires any chapter, household, or individual
to hold identical beliefs about the gods, spirits, ancestors, or nature of the sacred.
This Charter governs practice and institutional relationship. It does not govern
metaphysics.

## Article II — Foundational Principles

Every chapter affiliated with the EFT affirms the following ten principles as a
condition of affiliation. These principles are described more fully in Document II
(the Federation Protocol); they are stated here in summary as the constitutional core
of the tradition:

1. **Sacred Plurality** — the sacred may be legitimately encountered through
   multiple names, beings, symbols, and interpretations.
2. **Household Continuity** — the household is a sacred and social unit extending
   across generations.
3. **Ancestral Remembrance** — communities preserve memory of the dead and
   recognize inherited obligation.
4. **Local Stewardship** — communities cultivate real relationships with the actual
   places they inhabit.
5. **Reciprocity** — benefits received create reasonable obligations of return.
6. **Hospitality** — hosts and guests hold mutual, bounded duties toward one
   another.
7. **Personal Reputation** — conduct, not claimed status, is the basis of standing in
   the community.
8. **Seasonal Observance** — communities mark the recurring transitions of the
   natural and social year.
9. **Community Governance** — significant shared affairs are governed by an
   acknowledged deliberative body, not by unilateral authority.
10. **Interpretive Freedom** — ritual practice and theological interpretation are
    matters of local determination, within the bounds of this Charter.

## Article III — Theological Position

**Section 3.1.** The EFT holds the following as its complete and sufficient theological
position, and requires no other affirmation of belief from any member:

> Reality contains dimensions of value, meaning, and sacredness that human beings
> legitimately encounter through multiple inherited and locally developed traditions.
> We therefore cultivate reciprocal relationships with community, ancestry, place,
> nature, and the sacred, without requiring uniform metaphysical interpretation.

**Section 3.2.** No chapter, officer, or member may require another member to affirm
a specific metaphysical claim — including, but not limited to, claims about the literal
existence, nature, or historical origin of any deity, spirit, or mythological event — as a
condition of membership, participation, or good standing.

**Section 3.3.** Members and chapters remain free to hold and teach specific
theological positions of their own (literalist, archetypal, symbolic, agnostic, or
otherwise), provided such teaching is not imposed as a condition of federation
membership.

## Article IV — Structure of the Federation

**Section 4.1.** The EFT implements the **Federated Tradition Protocol (FTP)** through
this Charter and its accompanying governing documents. Within the EFT, any number
of **Distributions** may exist as regional, cultural, or thematic adaptations of the
European tradition (e.g., Norse, Celtic, Slavic, Hellenic, or Eclectic/Regional
Distributions).

**Section 4.2.** A Distribution may adapt calendar, ritual language, mythic material,
and cultural emphasis to its own tradition and locality, provided it does not violate
Article II, Article III, or Article VI of this Charter.

**Section 4.3.** A Chapter is a local community operating under a Distribution, or
directly under the EFT's general governing framework. A Chapter is the basic
self-governing unit of the Federation and shall adopt local bylaws consistent with
Document III.

**Section 4.4.** No Chapter, Distribution, or officer of the Federation possesses
authority over the internal theological life of any other Chapter. Authority under this
Charter is limited strictly to matters of shared governance, affiliation, and adherence
to the Foundational Principles and the Compatibility Blacklist (Article VI).

## Article V — Rights of Members

Every member of a Chapter affiliated with the EFT holds the following rights, which
no Chapter, officer, or Distribution may abridge:

**Section 5.1. Right of Belief.** No member may be compelled to affirm any specific
metaphysical claim.

**Section 5.2. Right of Voice.** Every member in good standing has the right to
participate in the Assembly of their Chapter, as defined in Document III.

**Section 5.3. Right of Exit.** Every member may leave a Chapter or the Federation at
any time, without spiritual threat, economic penalty, or social retaliation. Every
Chapter retains the equivalent right to disaffiliate from the Federation, and the
Federation retains the right to revoke a Chapter’s affiliation for violation of this
Charter, through the process defined in Document VII.

**Section 5.4. Right of Ancestry-Independent Participation.** No person may be
denied participation, office, or standing on the basis of ethnicity, ancestry, or national
origin. A Distribution may preserve and center a particular cultural inheritance
without restricting who may study, practice, or hold office within it.

**Section 5.5. Right of Due Process.** No member may be expelled, disciplined, or
stripped of office except through the procedure defined in Document VII.

## Article VI — The Compatibility Blacklist

**Section 6.1.** The following practices are incompatible with the EFT and may not be
adopted, taught, or practiced by any affiliated Chapter or Distribution, regardless of
claimed historical precedent:

- Human sacrifice
- Slavery or forced servitude
- Hereditary caste privilege
- Blood feud or sanctioned private vengeance
- Forced marriage
- Ethnic supremacy or ancestry-based exclusion
- Coercive conversion
- Religious violence
- Divination or ritual practice used to control an unwilling third party
- Absolute or unchallengeable priestly authority
- Discrimination based solely on ancestry

**Section 6.2.** This blacklist shall not be construed as a comprehensive historical
judgment on any ancient culture. It is a constraint on present-day practice within this
Federation.

**Section 6.3.** A Chapter found in persistent violation of this Article is subject to
disaffiliation under Document VII.

## Article VII — Limits on Institutional Authority

**Section 7.1.** No person may claim institutional authority — including control of
finances, property, membership, or governance — solely on the basis of claimed
supernatural revelation, personal spiritual experience, or claimed communication
with a deity, spirit, or ancestor.

**Section 7.2.** A member’s account of personal religious experience is protected
under Article V and may be freely shared, taught, and honored within a Chapter. It
does not, by itself, confer institutional office, override the Assembly, or exempt any
person from the governance and accountability structures defined in Document III
and Document VI (the Officer Manual).

**Section 7.3.** All institutional authority within a Chapter — including ritual office —
is conferred through the process defined in Document III, and remains subject to the
ordinary standards of accountability, term limits, and removal applicable to that
office.

## Article VIII — Amendment

**Section 8.1.** This Charter and the Foundational Principles of Article II constitute
the fixed constitutional core of the EFT and require the highest threshold of consent
to amend, as defined in Document II.

**Section 8.2.** Documents III through VII may be amended by the process each
document specifies for itself, provided no amendment contradicts this Charter.

**Section 8.3.** No amendment may narrow the rights guaranteed in Article V,
remove the Right of Exit (Section 5.3), remove the limits on institutional authority
(Article VII), or add to the Compatibility Blacklist any of the ten Foundational
Principles of Article II.

## Article IX — Ratification

This Charter takes effect for a given Chapter upon adoption of local bylaws consistent
with Document III, and upon that Chapter’s affirmation of the Foundational Principles
stated in Article II.
