# Document II — The Federation Protocol

## Full Elaboration of the Foundational Principles of the European Federated Tradition (EFT)

**Version 0.1 — Draft**

**Subordinate to Document I — The Charter. Where this Document and the Charter conflict, the Charter governs.**

## Preamble

Article II of the Charter states the ten Foundational Principles in summary. This
Document elaborates each principle into a working standard: what a Chapter must
actually do, keep, or refrain from doing to remain in good standing with the
Federation, and what is explicitly left open to local or Distribution-level discretion.

The governing logic throughout is the specification gradient stated in the Charter’s
spirit, restated here as a working rule:

> **Practice is required. Belief is not. Structure is specified. Content is local.**

Wherever this Document requires a Chapter to “maintain,” “observe,” or “preserve”
something, it is describing a minimum floor, not a ceiling. Chapters and Distributions
are free to exceed these minimums in whatever direction their tradition, locality, and
membership favor.

## Article I — Purpose and Status of This Document

**Section 1.1.** This Document exists to answer the question: what, specifically, must
a Chapter actually do to be a Chapter of the EFT, rather than merely an unaffiliated
group that happens to agree with the Charter?

**Section 1.2.** Compliance with this Document is a condition of affiliation. A
Chapter’s continued failure to meet a Minimum Standard defined below is grounds for
review under Document VII (Dispute Procedure) and, if unresolved, disaffiliation.

**Section 1.3.** Nothing in this Document may be interpreted to require a specific
theological affirmation, in accordance with Charter Article III.

## Article II — Sacred Plurality

**Principle.** The sacred may be legitimately encountered through multiple names,
beings, symbols, traditions, and interpretations. No single interpretation may be
imposed as orthodoxy.

**Minimum Standard.**

**2.1.** A Chapter may not require members to affirm a specific ontological claim
about any deity, spirit, or sacred power as a condition of participation, office, or good
standing.

**2.2.** A Chapter must permit members to hold and express, in appropriate settings,
a range of interpretive stances toward the sacred (literal, archetypal, symbolic,
agnostic, or otherwise) without penalty.

**2.3.** Mythic material (Primitive M1) is to be treated as cultural language conveying
identity, ethics, and cosmology — not as a historical or scientific claim requiring
affirmation. A Chapter may teach myth as sacred story without requiring belief in its
literal occurrence.

**Local Discretion.**

**2.4.** A Chapter or Distribution may center a specific mythic tradition (Norse,
Celtic, Slavic, Hellenic, etc.), teach it as its primary frame, and maintain internal
specialists in that tradition, provided Section 2.1 is respected.

## Article III — Household Continuity

**Principle.** The household is a sacred and social institution extending across
generations.

**Minimum Standard.**

**3.1.** A Chapter must provide its members with a framework — instruction, ritual
templates, or both — for marking at least the following household transitions: birth or
naming, coming of age, partnership/marriage, and death.

**3.2.** A Chapter must affirmatively recognize households of varying composition
(single persons, couples, families with or without children, chosen family,
multigenerational households) as equally valid sites of this Principle. No household
form may be treated as a precondition of good standing.

**Local Discretion.**

**3.3.** The specific content of household ritual (shrine practices, naming customs,
language used) is entirely a matter of household and Chapter choice.

## Article IV — Ancestral Remembrance

**Principle.** Communities preserve memory of the dead and recognize inherited
obligation.

**Minimum Standard.**

**4.1.** Each Chapter must maintain some durable record of its deceased members
and founders — a “Book of Names” or functional equivalent — and must observe at
least one remembrance rite per year in which that record is read or acknowledged.

**4.2.** “Ancestor” under this Article is not limited to blood kin. A Chapter must permit
members to honor cultural predecessors, teachers, and non-biological forebears under
this Principle.

**Local Discretion.**

**4.3.** The form of the remembrance rite, and any further ancestor practice beyond
the annual minimum, is local.

## Article V — Local Stewardship

**Principle.** Communities develop relationships with the actual places they inhabit.

**Minimum Standard.**

**5.1.** A Chapter must maintain at least one ongoing relationship with a specific
local place or ecological feature — a watershed, park, garden, tree, trail, or similar —
expressed through recurring stewardship activity (cleanup, planting, maintenance, or
monitoring) at least annually.

**5.2.** A Chapter’s calendar (Article IX) must reflect the actual seasonal and
ecological rhythm of its own region, rather than an imported climate or growing cycle
inconsistent with its members’ lived environment.

**Local Discretion.**

**5.3.** The choice of place, the nature of the stewardship activity, and its integration
into ritual life are local.

## Article VI — Reciprocity

**Principle.** Benefits received create reasonable obligations of return.

**Minimum Standard.**

**6.1.** A Chapter must maintain some recognized practice of offering (Primitive R3)
— the deliberate relinquishment of something of value (labor, resources, art, or
ecological work) in acknowledgment of a relationship or benefit received — observed
at least at the Chapter’s major seasonal gatherings.

**6.2.** A Chapter’s Ethical Code (Document V) must articulate reciprocity as a
standing obligation applicable to community, environment, and institution, not merely
as a private virtue.

**Local Discretion.**

**6.3.** The object and form of offering (material, ecological, financial, or
service-based) is local.

## Article VII — Hospitality

**Principle.** Hosts and guests hold mutual, bounded duties toward one another.

**Minimum Standard.**

**7.1.** A Chapter must maintain a written statement, available to members and
prospective members, of what a person may expect when attending a Chapter
gathering as a guest, and what is expected of them in turn.

**7.2.** Hospitality obligations under this Article are explicitly bounded: neither a host
nor a guest is obligated to unlimited or indefinite generosity, safety guarantees
beyond reasonable care, or continued hospitality toward a guest who has violated
Chapter norms.

**Local Discretion.**

**7.3.** The specific customs of welcome, gift-giving, and gathering etiquette are
local.

## Article VIII — Personal Reputation

**Principle.** Conduct, not claimed status, is the basis of standing in the community.

**Minimum Standard.**

**8.1.** No Chapter may confer or recognize standing, office, or authority on the basis
of claimed lineage, claimed spiritual gift, or claimed supernatural sanction alone (see
also Charter Article VII).

**8.2.** A Chapter’s process for conferring ritual office (Article XII, below) must be
based on observable conduct, demonstrated competence, or Assembly selection —
not solely on self-declaration.

**Local Discretion.**

**8.3.** The specific virtues emphasized (courage, generosity, truthfulness,
competence, etc.) and how they are recognized or celebrated are local.

## Article IX — Seasonal Observance

**Principle.** Communities mark recurring transitions in the natural and social year.

**Minimum Standard.**

**9.1.** A Chapter must observe no fewer than four seasonal gatherings per year,
corresponding to major transitions appropriate to its own region and Distribution.

**9.2.** These observances need not use any particular set of names (Norse, Celtic,
English, or invented) and need not align with any single ancient calendar.

**Local Discretion.**

**9.3.** Naming, additional festivals, and specific ritual content are local.

## Article X — Community Governance

**Principle.** Significant shared affairs are governed by an acknowledged deliberative
body, not unilateral authority.

**Minimum Standard.**

**10.1.** A Chapter must maintain an Assembly, constituted and operating as defined
in Document III, with real authority over Chapter finances, membership, and major
decisions.

**10.2.** A Chapter must maintain at least the offices of Treasurer and one Ritual
Office-holder (Article XII) as distinct roles; a single person may hold both only on a
temporary or emergency basis as defined in Document III.

**10.3.** A Chapter must maintain a dispute and removal process consistent with
Document VII.

**Local Discretion.**

**10.4.** The size, meeting frequency, and specific procedural rules of the Assembly
beyond the minimums in Document III are local.

## Article XI — Interpretive Freedom

**Principle.** Ritual practice and theological interpretation are matters of local
determination, within the bounds of the EFT Charter and this Federation Protocol.

**Minimum Standard.**

**11.1.** No Distribution or Chapter may prohibit a member from privately holding or
discussing a theological interpretation that differs from the Chapter’s primary
tradition, so long as the member does not claim Federation or Chapter authority for
that interpretation in violation of Article II above.

**Local Discretion.**

**11.2.** This Article is, by design, almost entirely local discretion. Its Minimum
Standard exists only to prevent Sections 2.1–2.2 from being locally overridden.

## Article XII — Ritual Office

*Supplementary institution supporting Article X.*

**Section 12.1.** A Ritual Office is a function, not a rank. Recognized offices may
include, without limitation: ritual leader, funeral celebrant, marriage officiant,
historian or storykeeper, treasurer, mediator, land steward, and festival coordinator.

**Section 12.2.** Holding a Ritual Office confers no authority beyond the specific
function of that office, and no Ritual Office-holder may invoke that office to override
the Assembly.

**Section 12.3.** Ritual Offices are conferred, term-limited, and removable as defined
in Document VI (Officer Manual) and Document III (Local Bylaws Template).

## Article XIII — Recognition of Distributions

**Section 13.1.** A Distribution is recognized by the Federation when it publishes:
(a) the specific cultural or regional tradition it draws upon, (b) any
Distribution-specific terminology or calendar, and (c) an affirmation that it operates
within the Foundational Principles and the Compatibility Blacklist (Charter Article VI).

**Section 13.2.** A Distribution may not restrict participation, study, or office within
it on the basis of the ancestry of a prospective member (Charter Section 5.4).

**Section 13.3.** New Distributions may be recognized by simple majority of existing
recognized Distributions’ representative delegates, or by whatever interim process
the founding Chapters establish prior to a full federation-level assembly existing.

## Article XIV — Amendment of the Foundational Principles

**Section 14.1.** Because Article II of the Charter is entrenched, no single Chapter or
Distribution may unilaterally alter, remove, or add to the ten Foundational Principles.

**Section 14.2.** Amendment of a Foundational Principle, or of the Minimum
Standards in this Document that implement it, requires the affirmative consent of a
supermajority (no less than two-thirds) of recognized Chapters across no fewer than
three recognized Distributions, following a public comment period of no less than
ninety days.

**Section 14.3.** No amendment adopted under this Article may narrow the rights
guaranteed in Charter Article V or weaken the limits on institutional authority in
Charter Article VII, per Charter Section 8.3.
