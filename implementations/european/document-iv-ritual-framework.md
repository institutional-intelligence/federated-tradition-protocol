# Document IV — The Ritual Framework

## Structural Minimums for Rite, Calendar, and Remembrance

**Version 0.1 — Draft**

**Subordinate to Document I — The Charter and Document II — The Federation Protocol.**

This Document specifies the required structure of Chapter ritual life — what a rite
must accomplish and acknowledge. It does not, and by design cannot, specify the
words, gestures, offerings, or mythic language used to accomplish it. That content
belongs to each Chapter and Distribution under Document II, Articles II and XI.

## Preamble

A rite is not a script. It is a structure that a community fills with its own language,
symbols, and inherited or invented material.

This Document exists to guarantee that every Chapter of the **European Federated
Tradition (EFT)** — whatever Distribution it belongs to, whatever names it uses for
its gods, seasons, or ancestors — provides its members with a functioning ritual life
that actually marks the transitions Document II requires: household passage, seasonal
change, and ancestral remembrance.

The rule throughout is the same rule that governs the rest of the Federation:

> **What a rite must accomplish is specified. What a rite says, and to whom, is not.**

## Article I — Purpose and Scope

**Section 1.1.** This Document implements Document II, Article III (Household
Continuity), Article IV (Ancestral Remembrance), Article VI (Reciprocity/Offering),
and Article IX (Seasonal Observance), at the level of actual ritual practice.

**Section 1.2.** A Chapter satisfies this Document by ensuring that each rite
described below occurs, in some form, and accomplishes the structural function
specified — regardless of the specific liturgy, deity-language, or cultural material
used.

**Section 1.3.** No provision of this Document may be read to require belief in the
literal efficacy or metaphysical reality of any rite. A rite may be performed, and may
be meaningful, under any of the interpretive stances protected in Document II,
Article II.

## Article II — Rites of Passage

Each rite of passage below has three required structural elements:

- **Acknowledgment** — the community formally recognizes the transition.
- **Threshold** — some marked action distinguishes before-state from after-state.
- **Witness** — the transition is witnessed by more than the person or persons
  undergoing it, and recorded where relevant.

Everything beyond these three elements is local.

### 2.1 — Birth or Naming Rite

**Acknowledgment:** the arrival of a new household member is formally recognized
by the Chapter.

**Threshold:** the child (or, for adult converts/new members joining a household
tradition, the individual) is given a name or formal welcome recognized by the
community.

**Witness:** at least one adult beyond the immediate household is present, and the
event is recorded in the Chapter’s household or membership records.

### 2.2 — Coming-of-Age Rite

**Acknowledgment:** the Chapter recognizes a member’s transition toward adult
standing.

**Threshold:** the rite must include some marked test, task, teaching, or vigil
distinguishing it from an ordinary gathering — its specific form is entirely local.

**Witness:** performed before, or later presented to, the Assembly or an equivalent
gathering of adult members.

### 2.3 — Partnership / Marriage Rite

**Acknowledgment:** the Chapter formally recognizes a committed partnership, in
whatever form its members define partnership. The Federation imposes no restriction
on the number, gender configuration, or legal status of partners; see Charter Article V.

**Threshold:** an exchange, vow, or binding gesture marks the moment of formal
partnership.

**Witness:** performed before witnesses and recorded in Chapter records.

### 2.4 — Death / Funeral Rite

**Acknowledgment:** the Chapter formally marks the death of a member, and, where
the family requests it, of a non-member connected to the Chapter.

**Threshold:** some marked act of farewell or release distinguishes the funeral rite
from ordinary mourning.

**Witness:** the deceased’s name is entered into the Chapter’s Book of Names
(Article IV, below) at or before the next Remembrance Rite.

**Section 2.5.** A Chapter may add further rites of passage (e.g., adoption into a
household, retirement, gender transition, recovery, return from long absence) using
the same three-element structure.

## Article III — Seasonal Calendar

**Section 3.1. Required Minimum.** A Chapter shall observe no fewer than four
seasonal gatherings per year (Document II, Section 9.1), corresponding to the
following functional themes, in whatever order its actual regional seasons occur:

| Functional Theme | Structural Content |
| --- | --- |
| **Turning Inward** | remembrance, household focus, rest |
| **Turning Outward** | renewal, beginnings, new undertakings |
| **Gathering** | abundance, community strength, shared labor or celebration |
| **Closing** | harvest, gratitude, preparation for the next turning |

**Section 3.2.** These four functional themes are a structural scaffold, not a required
naming convention. A Distribution may map them onto its own traditional festival
names (e.g., a Norse Distribution may use Yule, Ostara, Midsummer, and
Harvest-equivalent names; a Southern Hemisphere or non-European-climate Chapter
may reorder or rename them to match its actual seasons).

**Section 3.3.** A Chapter is free to add further observances beyond the required
four.

## Article IV — Ancestral Remembrance Rite

**Section 4.1.** Each Chapter shall maintain a **Book of Names** — a durable,
added-to record of its deceased members, founders, and any other figures the Chapter
chooses to honor under Document II, Section 4.2 (including non-biological forebears,
teachers, and cultural predecessors).

**Section 4.2.** No less than once per year, ordinarily at the “Turning Inward”
seasonal gathering (Article III), the Chapter shall hold a Remembrance Rite in which
the Book of Names is read aloud, in whole or in relevant part, before the assembled
community.

**Section 4.3.** The specific form of remembrance — candles, offerings, storytelling,
silence, or other practice — is local.

## Article V — Offering Practice

**Section 5.1.** A Chapter shall include a structured act of offering — the deliberate
relinquishment of something of value in acknowledgment of relationship or benefit
received (Document II, Article VI) — at each of its four required seasonal gatherings
(Article III) at minimum.

**Section 5.2.** An offering under this Article must be **directed**: it must name what
or whom it is offered toward (a place, an ancestor, a deity, a community, or the group
itself); and **realized**: it must involve an actual relinquishment (of labor, resources,
time, or goods), not merely a symbolic gesture without cost.

**Section 5.3.** Ecological or community labor (a river cleanup, a repair project, a
charitable contribution) satisfies this Article as fully as a traditional material offering,
and is explicitly encouraged as a modern realization of Local Stewardship (Document
II, Article V) and Offering together.

## Article VI — Household Ritual

**Section 6.1.** This Document imposes no mandatory minimum on private household
practice beyond the rites of passage in Article II. Household Continuity (Document II,
Article III) is understood to be substantially fulfilled through ordinary household life
— recognition of household members, informal remembrance, and hospitality — and
is not required to take a specific ritual form.

**Section 6.2.** A Chapter may, but is not required to, provide households with
optional templates for a household shrine, daily or seasonal household observance,
or similar practice, for members who wish to formalize their household ritual life.

## Article VII — Role of Ritual Office-Holders

**Section 7.1.** Where a Chapter has designated a Ritual Office-holder under
Document II, Article XII, that person or persons ordinarily lead or coordinate the rites
in this Document, but their role is functional, not authoritative: leading a rite confers
no standing beyond the rite itself, and does not exempt the office-holder from the
limits of Document III, Article III, Section 3.5.

**Section 7.2.** In the absence of a designated Ritual Office-holder, any member or
members the Chapter chooses may lead a given rite. No rite in this Document requires
an ordained or singularly authorized officiant to be valid.

## Article VIII — Distribution-Level Elaboration

**Section 8.1.** A Distribution (Document II, Article XIII) is expected to publish its
own **Ritual Companion** — a non-binding elaboration of this Document providing
specific liturgical language, festival names, mythic material, and household practices
consistent with its tradition.

**Section 8.2.** A Distribution’s Ritual Companion is not part of the binding
governing documents of the EFT and may be freely revised by that Distribution,
provided the underlying structure required by this Document continues to be met.

**Section 8.3.** A Chapter without an available Distribution-specific Ritual
Companion (e.g., a newly forming Eclectic or Regional Chapter) may draft its own,
or adopt another Distribution’s in whole or adapted part, without penalty.
