# Document V — Ethical Code

## Kitsune-Kyō
### An Implementation of the Federated Tradition Protocol

**Version 0.1**

---

## Preamble

Kitsune-Kyō does not begin with a comprehensive list of commandments.

Its source tradition instead presents a constellation of virtues, symbols, stories, and practices centered upon freedom, adaptability, resilience, loyalty, joy, transformation, remembrance, renewal, and the creative conjunction of apparent opposites.

This Ethical Code develops those themes into guidance for communal life while also establishing institutional safeguards necessary for healthy Local Shrines and federated institutions.

It therefore distinguishes between:

- **religious virtues**, derived from or developed through Kitsune-Kyō;
- **community ethics**, governing relationships among participants; and
- **institutional duties**, arising from positions of authority, stewardship, and trust.

Not every ethical question has a predetermined answer.

Kitsune-Kyō seeks not to eliminate judgment but to cultivate people and institutions capable of exercising it responsibly.

---

## Article I — The Way of Kitsune

### Section 1. Adaptability

The fox survives by observing, learning, and adapting.

Followers of Kitsune-Kyō are encouraged to respond intelligently to changing circumstances rather than treating rigidity as a virtue in itself.

Adaptability does not require abandonment of principle.

It means learning how principles may be preserved under changing conditions.

### Section 2. Curiosity

Mystery should invite inquiry rather than fear.

Members are encouraged to:

- ask questions;
- examine assumptions;
- explore unfamiliar ideas;
- learn from experience;
- and remain capable of changing their minds.

### Section 3. Playfulness

Playfulness, humor, imagination, and creative experimentation are legitimate parts of a meaningful life.

Serious things need not always be approached solemnly.

### Section 4. Cunning

Cunning in the Way of Kitsune means perceptiveness, strategic intelligence, and the ability to recognize appearances that conceal underlying realities.

It does not authorize fraud, exploitation, or deliberate abuse of trust.

### Section 5. Freedom

Kitsune-Kyō values personal freedom and resistance to unnecessary constraint.

Freedom is strongest when joined to responsibility for the foreseeable consequences of one's choices.

---

## Article II — The Metal Spirit

### Section 1. Resilience

Difficulty, failure, rejection, loss, and uncertainty are part of human life.

The Metal Spirit encourages the capacity to continue, rebuild, adapt, and create after disruption.

### Section 2. Courage

Courage does not require the absence of fear.

It is the willingness to act appropriately despite difficulty, uncertainty, embarrassment, or risk.

### Section 3. Expression

Music, art, movement, performance, writing, and other forms of expression may transform experiences that are difficult to communicate directly.

Members are encouraged to create rather than merely consume.

### Section 4. Catharsis

Intensity may be constructive.

Music, ritual, movement, humor, and communal participation may provide legitimate means for expressing and transforming powerful emotions.

Catharsis does not excuse harming other people.

### Section 5. Perseverance

Persistence is honored, but persistence is not identical to refusing to change course.

Sometimes perseverance means continuing.

Sometimes it means adapting.

Sometimes it means ending what no longer works and beginning again.

---

## Article III — Unity of Light and Dark

### Section 1. Complexity

People and circumstances rarely fit perfectly into simple categories.

Kitsune-Kyō encourages the capacity to recognize complexity without abandoning the ability to make distinctions.

### Section 2. Opposites

Strength and gentleness, joy and grief, seriousness and play, individuality and fellowship, continuity and change may coexist.

One need not destroy the other.

### Section 3. Shadow

Members should be willing to recognize uncomfortable aspects of themselves, their communities, and their institutions.

Ignoring weakness does not create strength.

### Section 4. Celebration and Mourning

A healthy community must possess language for both celebration and loss.

Neither should invalidate the other.

### Section 5. Uncertainty

Not knowing is sometimes an honest answer.

**Only the Fox God knows** may express humility before genuine mystery.

It shall not be used as an excuse to avoid questions for which an institution actually possesses records, responsibilities, or evidence.

---

## Article IV — Loyalty

### Section 1. Fellowship

Kitsune-Kyō values loyalty among people who share community, responsibility, and experience.

### Section 2. Loyalty and Truth

Loyalty does not require dishonesty.

A person does not serve a friend, officer, Shrine, or tradition well by concealing serious misconduct merely to protect reputation.

### Section 3. Loyalty and Conscience

Institutional loyalty does not require surrender of conscience.

Members may question leaders, policies, interpretations, and institutional decisions without automatically becoming disloyal to Kitsune-Kyō.

### Section 4. Departure

A person who leaves a Local Shrine or Kitsune-Kyō should not automatically be treated as an enemy, traitor, or object of contempt.

### Section 5. Institutional Loyalty

Officers owe loyalty to the responsibilities of their offices, not merely to the individuals who helped place them there.

---

## Article V — Joy

### Section 1. Joy as a Good

Joy is not frivolous merely because suffering exists.

Celebration, friendship, humor, music, food, dance, art, and shared experiences may be treated as genuine goods.

### Section 2. The Smiling Warrior

Strength and joy need not be opposites.

A person may confront difficulty without allowing difficulty to define the whole of life.

### Section 3. Community

Local Shrines should cultivate opportunities for members to enjoy one another's company beyond formal governance and obligation.

### Section 4. No Compulsory Cheerfulness

Joy shall not become an institutional demand that members conceal grief, anger, exhaustion, disagreement, or distress.

There is room in Kitsune-Kyō for both the smile and the shadow.

---

## Article VI — Renewal

### Section 1. Transformation

People and institutions can change.

Past identity does not permanently determine future possibility.

### Section 2. Failure

Failure should be examined for what it can teach.

Where repair is possible, communities should permit meaningful correction rather than treating every mistake as permanently disqualifying.

### Section 3. Accountability

Renewal does not mean pretending that harm never occurred.

Forgiveness, reconciliation, restoration of trust, restoration to membership, and restoration to office are distinct questions and need not occur together.

### Section 4. New Beginnings

Kitsune-Kyō honors the possibility of beginning again after loss, disruption, or transformation.

### Section 5. Institutional Renewal

A Shrine may reform structures that no longer serve their intended purpose.

Tradition is preserved through intelligent continuity, not institutional paralysis.

---

## Article VII — Human Dignity and Voluntary Participation

### Section 1. Dignity

Participants shall be treated as persons rather than merely as resources for an institution.

### Section 2. Consent

Participation in Kitsune-Kyō is voluntary.

Religious authority shall not be used to coerce:

- membership;
- financial contributions;
- romantic or sexual relationships;
- labor;
- political activity;
- disclosure of private information;
- or participation in activities unrelated to legitimate institutional responsibilities.

### Section 3. Boundaries

Members and officers should respect reasonable personal, physical, emotional, and social boundaries.

### Section 4. Harassment

Harassment, stalking, intimidation, threats, and deliberate humiliation are inconsistent with healthy community life.

### Section 5. Retaliation

No person should suffer institutional retaliation merely for making a good-faith report of misconduct, raising a governance concern, requesting financial accountability, or participating honestly in a dispute process.

---

## Article VIII — Religious Authority

### Section 1. Authority as Service

Religious office exists to serve the community and tradition.

It is not evidence that the officeholder possesses greater human worth.

### Section 2. Claims of Divine Favor

No person acquires institutional authority merely by claiming:

- revelation;
- prophecy;
- mystical experience;
- communication with the Fox God;
- unusual spiritual insight;
- sacred destiny;
- or special favor from a sacred figure.

Institutional authority arises through institutional procedures.

### Section 3. Spiritual Manipulation

A religious officer shall not knowingly use claims of divine judgment, spiritual danger, ritual impurity, supernatural punishment, or privileged revelation as tools for personal coercion or exploitation.

### Section 4. Questions

Members may question religious officers.

Respect for an office does not require intellectual submission to every opinion of the person holding it.

### Section 5. Symbolic Status

Ceremonial prominence does not create unchecked administrative authority.

---

## Article IX — Leadership and Power

### Section 1. Fiduciary Spirit

Even where the law does not technically impose a fiduciary classification, officers should treat institutional authority as a position of trust.

### Section 2. Conflicts of Interest

Officers shall disclose substantial conflicts of interest relevant to decisions under their authority.

### Section 3. Recusal

Where an officer's personal interest materially conflicts with impartial decision-making, recusal should ordinarily occur.

### Section 4. Nepotism and Favoritism

Personal friendship, family relationships, romantic relationships, celebrity, popularity, wealth, or artistic prominence shall not excuse officers from ordinary institutional procedures.

### Section 5. Concentration of Power

Local Shrines should avoid unnecessary concentration of financial, disciplinary, electoral, and recordkeeping authority in a single individual.

### Section 6. Criticism

Good-faith criticism of an officer shall not itself constitute misconduct.

---

## Article X — Financial Ethics

### Section 1. Stewardship

Communal money shall be used for communal purposes consistent with the authorization under which it was received.

### Section 2. Transparency

Institutions shall maintain financial records appropriate to their size and activities.

### Section 3. Personal Benefit

Officers shall not treat institutional funds, property, accounts, or opportunities as personal possessions.

### Section 4. Donations

Donations should be voluntary.

Religious status shall not be conditioned upon wealth or the ability to contribute money except where a transparent fee is reasonably necessary for a particular optional activity.

### Section 5. Compensation

Reasonable compensation for legitimate work is not inconsistent with religious service.

Compensation should be authorized transparently and with appropriate attention to conflicts of interest.

### Section 6. Restricted Gifts

Funds accepted for a specific purpose should be used consistently with that purpose or handled according to applicable law and donor agreements.

---

## Article XI — Sexual and Romantic Ethics

### Section 1. Consent

Sexual and romantic relationships require meaningful consent.

Religious status does not override another person's boundaries.

### Section 2. Power Differences

Religious officers and institutional leaders should recognize that differences in authority may complicate consent and create conflicts of interest.

### Section 3. Exploitation

An officer shall not use spiritual authority, confidential information, institutional dependency, or threats of religious consequences to obtain romantic or sexual access.

### Section 4. Institutional Decisions

Where a romantic, sexual, or intimate relationship creates a substantial conflict of interest, appropriate disclosure, recusal, reassignment, or other safeguards should be used.

### Section 5. Privacy

Consensual private relationships between adults are not automatically matters for institutional supervision merely because the participants belong to the same Shrine.

---

## Article XII — Privacy and Confidentiality

### Section 1. Personal Information

Institutions should collect only personal information reasonably related to legitimate activities.

### Section 2. Confidential Communications

A person entrusted with confidential information should not disclose it casually or use it for personal advantage.

### Section 3. Limits

Confidentiality shall not be represented as absolute where disclosure is required by law or reasonably necessary to address imminent danger or other serious circumstances.

### Section 4. Records

Sensitive membership, disciplinary, financial, or pastoral records shall receive appropriate protection.

### Section 5. Photography and Recording

Participation in a ritual does not automatically constitute consent to unrestricted publication of a participant's image, voice, or personal information.

---

## Article XIII — Creativity and Intellectual Honesty

### Section 1. Creation

Kitsune-Kyō encourages creation.

Members may write music, rituals, stories, commentaries, visual art, scholarship, translations, and other works inspired by the tradition.

### Section 2. Attribution

Creators should receive appropriate attribution where authorship is known and attribution is reasonably possible.

### Section 3. Provenance

New material should not knowingly be represented as ancient, inherited, revealed, official, or universally binding when it is not.

### Section 4. Interpretation

Members may offer interpretations of Kitsune-Kyō while distinguishing personal interpretation from established institutional positions.

### Section 5. Third-Party Works

Religious admiration does not create ownership of another person's creative work.

Kitsune-Kyō institutions should respect applicable rights associated with music, images, recordings, trademarks, writings, performances, and other protected works.

---

## Article XIV — Disagreement

### Section 1. Good-Faith Difference

Members may disagree concerning theology, mythology, ritual, aesthetics, governance, and interpretation.

Disagreement alone does not constitute misconduct.

### Section 2. Argument

Ideas may be criticized vigorously.

Personal intimidation, harassment, or abuse should not be confused with vigorous intellectual disagreement.

### Section 3. Interpretation

No member shall falsely represent a personal interpretation as the settled position of the federation or a Local Shrine.

### Section 4. Institutional Questions

When a disagreement concerns an institutional decision rather than an abstract idea, the applicable governance or dispute procedure should be used.

### Section 5. Departure and Schism

Where disagreement becomes irreconcilable, peaceful institutional separation is preferable to indefinite struggles for domination.

Federation should make separation possible without requiring destruction of either community.

---

## Article XV — Safety

### Section 1. Reasonable Care

Shrines shall exercise reasonable care in organizing physical activities.

### Section 2. Music and Events

Concert-style ritual may involve volume, darkness, movement, crowds, theatrical effects, or strenuous participation.

Organizers should consider foreseeable risks appropriate to the circumstances.

### Section 3. Voluntary Physical Participation

No participant shall be required to enter a mosh pit, headbang, dance, kneel, stand for prolonged periods, wear a mask, or perform another physical act as a condition of ordinary belonging.

### Section 4. Accessibility

Reasonable alternatives should be considered where physical ability or sensory needs affect participation.

### Section 5. Emergencies

Emergency needs take priority over ceremonial form.

---

## Article XVI — Children and Vulnerable Participants

### Section 1. Protection

The safety of children and vulnerable participants takes priority over the prestige or convenience of officers and institutions.

### Section 2. Appropriate Conduct

Local Shrines shall maintain reasonable expectations concerning adult conduct around minors.

### Section 3. Authority

Religious office does not grant unrestricted access to children or vulnerable participants.

### Section 4. Reporting

Institutions and officers shall comply with applicable legal reporting obligations.

### Section 5. Formation

Religious formation of younger participants should be age-appropriate and should not depend upon fear, humiliation, or manipulation.

---

## Article XVII — Accountability and Correction

### Section 1. No Exemption by Status

No founder, officer, Metal Priestess, Kitsune Knight, donor, artist, charismatic personality, long-serving member, or other participant is exempt from applicable institutional rules merely because of status.

### Section 2. Proportionality

Responses to misconduct should take account of:

- seriousness;
- intent where relevant;
- harm;
- repetition;
- acceptance of responsibility;
- possibility of correction;
- and the need to protect others.

### Section 3. Due Process

Serious allegations should be addressed through fair procedures rather than rumor, popularity contests, or unilateral declarations.

### Section 4. Restoration

Where appropriate, a person who has violated community standards may be restored to participation after meaningful correction.

Restoration to a position of authority is a separate determination.

### Section 5. Records

Serious institutional actions should be documented sufficiently to preserve accountability and continuity.

---

## Article XVIII — Relationship Between Ethics and Discipline

### Section 1. Aspirational and Enforceable Standards

Not every ethical ideal in this Code creates grounds for formal discipline.

Some provisions describe virtues toward which members are encouraged to grow.

Others establish minimum expectations necessary for institutional trust.

### Section 2. Interpretation

When disciplinary consequences are possible, rules should be interpreted with sufficient clarity that participants can reasonably understand the conduct expected of them.

### Section 3. Procedure

Formal discipline shall proceed according to Document VII — Dispute Procedure and applicable Local Shrine bylaws.

### Section 4. Local Standards

Local Shrines may adopt additional ethical expectations consistent with this Code.

---

## Article XIX — Ethical Development

### Section 1. Living Tradition

The ethical thought of Kitsune-Kyō may develop as communities gain experience.

### Section 2. Commentary

Local Shrines, officers, and individual members may produce ethical commentary.

Commentary should identify its status and authorship rather than automatically claiming universal authority.

### Section 3. Case Experience

Institutional experience may reveal situations not adequately addressed by this Code.

Future revisions should learn from actual cases while avoiding unnecessary regulation of ordinary life.

### Section 4. Amendment

Federated amendments to this Code shall follow the procedures established by the Charter and Federation Protocol.

---

## Closing Principle

The fox survives through adaptability.

The Metal Spirit survives through resilience.

Community survives through loyalty.

Joy gives community a reason to survive.

And none of these virtues remain virtues when they become excuses for domination, deception, exploitation, or fear.

Kitsune-Kyō therefore asks its members not merely to follow rules, but to cultivate judgment:

to know when to stand firm,

when to adapt,

when to laugh,

when to mourn,

when to forgive,

when to leave,

and when to begin again.

**Walk lightly. Stand loudly when necessary. Keep the flame.**
