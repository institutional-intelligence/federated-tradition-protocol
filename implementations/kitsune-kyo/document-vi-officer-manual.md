# Document VI — Officer Manual

## Kitsune-Kyō
### An Implementation of the Federated Tradition Protocol

**Version 0.1**

---

## Preamble

Institutions endure when responsibilities can pass from one person to another without the institution having to be reinvented.

Kitsune-Kyō recognizes religious, administrative, and service roles. Its source tradition identifies Metal Priestesses, Kitsune Knights, and Acolytes of the Shrine, while the institutional framework created under the Federated Tradition Protocol requires additional administrative offices for governance, records, finance, coordination, and continuity.

This Manual defines those roles without confusing religious symbolism with institutional power.

An office is a responsibility.

A title is not ownership.

No officer owns Kitsune-Kyō, a Local Shrine, the federation, its members, its records, or its assets merely by holding office.

---

# Part I — General Principles of Office

## Article I — Nature of Office

### Section 1. Service

Office exists for the benefit of the institution and the community it serves.

An officer receives defined responsibilities and authority necessary to perform them.

### Section 2. Defined Authority

The powers of an office arise from:

- the Charter;
- the Federation Protocol;
- Local Shrine bylaws;
- this Officer Manual;
- properly adopted policies;
- and lawful decisions of the body possessing authority over the office.

Authority shall not be enlarged merely through custom, personality, popularity, longevity, or assertion.

### Section 3. Religious and Administrative Authority

Religious authority and administrative authority are distinct.

A person may hold:

- a religious office;
- an administrative office;
- both;
- or neither.

Holding one does not automatically confer the other.

### Section 4. Temporary Custody

Officeholders are temporary custodians of institutional responsibilities.

Records, accounts, passwords, ceremonial property, archives, equipment, and other institutional resources shall be transferred appropriately when an officer leaves office.

### Section 5. No Founder Exception

Founders are subject to the same governing documents as other participants unless a specific provision expressly establishes otherwise.

Founding an institution does not create permanent ownership of its offices.

---

## Article II — Standards for All Officers

### Section 1. Good Faith

Officers shall perform their duties in good faith and with reasonable care.

### Section 2. Governing Documents

Officers should understand the governing documents relevant to their responsibilities.

### Section 3. Conflicts of Interest

An officer shall disclose substantial personal interests that could materially affect institutional judgment.

Recusal should occur where appropriate.

### Section 4. Confidentiality

Access to confidential information exists for institutional purposes.

It shall not be used for gossip, retaliation, personal leverage, or unrelated advantage.

### Section 5. Records

Material actions taken in an official capacity should be documented sufficiently for institutional continuity.

### Section 6. Communication

Officers should communicate information necessary for other responsible persons to perform their duties.

Institutional knowledge should not be deliberately hoarded as a source of personal power.

### Section 7. Conduct

Officers are subject to the Kitsune-Kyō Ethical Code.

---

# Part II — Local Shrine Religious Offices

## Article III — Metal Priestess

### Section 1. Character of the Office

The Metal Priestess is a religious office concerned primarily with ritual, teaching, formation, and stewardship of the religious life of a Local Shrine.

The office develops the source tradition's conception of Metal Priestesses as leaders of ritual and performance.

### Section 2. Responsibilities

A Metal Priestess may:

- lead or coordinate ceremonies;
- teach Kitsune-Kyō traditions;
- explain ritual symbolism;
- mentor Acolytes;
- assist in the development of new rites;
- maintain ceremonial standards;
- coordinate festivals and observances;
- support members during significant life events;
- and contribute to preservation of local religious traditions.

### Section 3. Authority

A Metal Priestess possesses authority necessary to perform the responsibilities assigned to the office.

The title alone does not confer unilateral authority over:

- Shrine finances;
- institutional property;
- membership records;
- elections;
- amendments;
- discipline;
- contracts;
- or the Local Council.

Such authority must arise independently through the governing documents.

### Section 4. Qualifications

A candidate should demonstrate:

- substantial familiarity with Kitsune-Kyō;
- knowledge of important rites and symbols;
- ability to conduct or coordinate ritual;
- sound judgment;
- respect for participant boundaries;
- ability to distinguish inherited tradition from later innovation;
- and willingness to remain accountable to institutional procedures.

### Section 5. Formation

Preparation may include:

- study of the Kitsune-Kyō source tradition;
- study of the governing documents;
- ritual observation;
- supervised ritual leadership;
- instruction in ethics and boundaries;
- practical event preparation;
- and mentorship.

### Section 6. Recognition

A Local Shrine shall establish a documented procedure for recognizing a Metal Priestess.

Federated standards may establish additional requirements for credentials intended to receive recognition outside the issuing Shrine.

### Section 7. Gender and Title

The inherited title is **Metal Priestess**.

Local Shrines may determine how the office is applied in circumstances not addressed by the source tradition, provided that local adaptations are identified as such.

---

## Article IV — Kitsune Knight

### Section 1. Character of the Office

The Kitsune Knight is a service role concerned with hospitality, practical assistance, ceremonial order, and participant safety.

### Section 2. Responsibilities

A Kitsune Knight may assist with:

- welcoming participants;
- preparing ritual space;
- procession and ceremonial logistics;
- accessibility;
- crowd movement;
- event safety;
- equipment;
- emergency coordination;
- protection of ceremonial objects;
- and orderly conclusion of gatherings.

### Section 3. Limits

A Kitsune Knight is not a police officer merely by virtue of the title.

The office does not confer authority to:

- detain people unlawfully;
- search personal belongings without lawful justification or consent;
- use unnecessary force;
- threaten participants;
- or punish members.

### Section 4. De-escalation

Kitsune Knights should favor calm communication and de-escalation where circumstances permit.

### Section 5. Safety

Where an immediate safety problem exists, protection of people takes priority over preservation of ceremonial form.

### Section 6. Training

Depending upon Shrine activities, useful training may include:

- event safety;
- first aid;
- accessibility;
- de-escalation;
- emergency procedures;
- and venue-specific requirements.

No training credential shall be claimed unless actually obtained.

---

## Article V — Acolyte of the Shrine

### Section 1. Character

An Acolyte is a participant undertaking structured learning, service, or preparation for greater responsibility.

### Section 2. Purpose

Acolyte formation may provide experience in:

- Kitsune-Kyō history;
- mythology;
- ritual;
- music;
- ethics;
- institutional governance;
- service;
- and community life.

### Section 3. Mentorship

Where practical, an Acolyte should have access to one or more experienced members or officers who can assist with formation.

### Section 4. No Guaranteed Promotion

Acolyte status does not guarantee eventual recognition as a Metal Priestess, Kitsune Knight, administrative officer, or other officeholder.

### Section 5. No Exploitation

Acolyte formation shall not be used as a pretext for unlimited unpaid labor, personal service to officers, financial extraction, humiliation, or coercive control.

### Section 6. Completion

A Shrine may recognize completion of an Acolyte program through an appropriate ceremony or institutional record.

---

# Part III — Local Shrine Administrative Offices

## Article VI — Presiding Officer

### Section 1. Purpose

The Presiding Officer coordinates the governance functions of the Local Shrine.

The Shrine may adopt another title for this office.

### Section 2. Responsibilities

The Presiding Officer may:

- convene governance meetings;
- prepare agendas;
- facilitate deliberation;
- ensure adopted decisions are assigned for implementation;
- coordinate officers;
- serve as a principal institutional contact;
- and perform other duties assigned by the bylaws.

### Section 3. Limits

The Presiding Officer is not the owner or supreme authority of the Shrine.

Presiding over meetings does not create unilateral authority to reverse decisions properly made by the membership or Local Council.

### Section 4. Religious Status

The Presiding Officer need not be a Metal Priestess unless local bylaws expressly require it.

---

## Article VII — Records Officer

### Section 1. Purpose

The Records Officer maintains the institutional memory of the Shrine.

### Section 2. Core Responsibilities

The Records Officer shall maintain or coordinate maintenance of:

- current bylaws;
- amendment history;
- significant meeting minutes;
- resolutions;
- officer rosters;
- necessary membership records;
- federation correspondence;
- recognition records;
- significant agreements;
- and other materials required for institutional continuity.

### Section 3. Minutes

Minutes should record decisions rather than attempt to transcribe every spoken word.

They should ordinarily identify:

- date;
- participating decision-making body;
- quorum where relevant;
- motions or questions decided;
- results;
- and responsibilities created by the decision.

### Section 4. Access

Institutional records should be made available to persons entitled to inspect them while protecting legitimately confidential material.

### Section 5. Continuity

Records shall be stored in a manner reasonably designed to survive the departure or incapacity of a single officer.

---

## Article VIII — Financial Officer

### Section 1. Purpose

The Financial Officer maintains oversight and records concerning Shrine finances.

The Shrine may use titles such as Treasurer or another locally selected designation.

### Section 2. Responsibilities

Responsibilities may include:

- maintaining financial records;
- receiving or documenting income;
- paying authorized expenses;
- preparing financial reports;
- assisting with budgets;
- maintaining account information;
- preserving receipts and supporting documentation;
- and coordinating legally required financial filings where applicable.

### Section 3. Authorization

Maintaining financial accounts does not itself authorize expenditures.

Expenditures shall follow the authorization procedures established by the bylaws or adopted financial policies.

### Section 4. Separation of Functions

Where reasonably practical, financial systems should require more than one person to participate in significant financial control.

### Section 5. Reporting

The Financial Officer shall provide reports according to the Local Shrine bylaws and upon reasonable request of the body responsible for financial oversight.

### Section 6. Transition

Upon leaving office, the Financial Officer shall promptly transfer institutional funds, records, credentials, account access, and other Shrine property to the authorized successor.

---

## Article IX — Additional Local Offices

### Section 1. Creation

A Local Shrine may create additional offices appropriate to its needs.

These may include responsibility for:

- communications;
- music;
- archives;
- technology;
- hospitality;
- education;
- events;
- charitable activity;
- accessibility;
- facilities;
- or other functions.

### Section 2. Written Duties

The duties and authority of a continuing office should be documented.

### Section 3. Avoiding Title Inflation

A title should correspond to an actual responsibility.

Creating elaborate titles without defined functions may obscure rather than strengthen institutional structure.

---

# Part IV — Federated Offices

## Article X — Federation Coordinator

### Section 1. Purpose

The Federation Coordinator facilitates the ordinary administration of the Kitsune-Kyō Federation.

### Section 2. Responsibilities

The Coordinator may:

- convene or facilitate meetings of the Council of Shrines;
- coordinate implementation of Council decisions;
- maintain communication among Local Shrines;
- assist committees and working groups;
- oversee routine federation administration;
- and represent the federation where properly authorized.

### Section 3. Limits

The Coordinator does not govern Local Shrines.

The office possesses no authority not delegated through the governing documents or Council decisions.

### Section 4. Public Representation

When speaking publicly in an official capacity, the Coordinator should distinguish:

- adopted federation positions;
- matters under deliberation;
- local positions;
- and personal opinions.

---

## Article XI — Federation Archivist

### Section 1. Purpose

The Federation Archivist preserves the institutional memory of the federation.

### Section 2. Responsibilities

The Archivist may maintain:

- governing documents;
- version histories;
- Council records;
- recognition records;
- historical materials;
- ritual resources;
- translations;
- publications;
- and other federated archives.

### Section 3. Provenance

The Archivist should preserve information concerning the origin and status of materials where reasonably possible.

### Section 4. Historical Integrity

Superseded materials should ordinarily be archived rather than silently rewritten or destroyed.

The history of institutional development is itself part of the record.

---

## Article XII — Federation Financial Officer

### Section 1. Purpose

The Federation Financial Officer administers federated finances under Council oversight.

### Section 2. Responsibilities

The office shall maintain appropriate:

- account records;
- transaction records;
- budgets;
- receipts;
- financial reports;
- and other documentation.

### Section 3. Reporting

Material financial information shall be reported periodically to the Council of Shrines.

### Section 4. Independence

Federated funds shall remain distinct from the personal finances of officers and from the independent finances of Local Shrines.

---

## Article XIII — Other Federated Officers

The Council of Shrines may establish additional offices for functions including:

- publications;
- translation;
- training;
- ritual resources;
- communications;
- mediation;
- technology;
- events;
- and international coordination.

Each continuing office shall have documented:

- responsibilities;
- authority;
- method of selection;
- term or service condition;
- accountability;
- and removal procedure.

---

# Part V — Selection, Terms, and Succession

## Article XIV — Selection

### Section 1. Procedure

Officers shall be selected according to the governing document applicable to the office.

### Section 2. Eligibility

Eligibility requirements should relate reasonably to the responsibilities of the office.

### Section 3. Disclosure

Candidates should disclose material conflicts or circumstances that would substantially interfere with performance of the office.

### Section 4. Contested Selection

Where more than one candidate seeks an elected office, the selection process should provide a fair means of choosing among them.

---

## Article XV — Terms

### Section 1. Defined Service

Administrative offices should ordinarily have defined terms or clearly defined service conditions.

### Section 2. Renewal

An officer may serve additional terms where permitted.

Repeated selection does not transform a temporary office into personal property.

### Section 3. Holdover

Where a successor has not yet been selected, bylaws may permit a limited holdover period necessary for continuity.

Holdover status should not be used to evade required selection procedures.

---

## Article XVI — Vacancies and Incapacity

### Section 1. Vacancy

A vacancy occurs upon:

- resignation;
- removal;
- death;
- permanent incapacity;
- expiration without authorized holdover;
- or another event defined by the governing documents.

### Section 2. Temporary Incapacity

Where an officer is temporarily unable to serve, an acting officer may be appointed according to applicable procedures.

### Section 3. Emergency Continuity

Local Shrines and the federation should identify who may perform essential functions if key officers suddenly become unavailable.

### Section 4. No Automatic Succession by Relationship

A spouse, partner, relative, friend, student, or protégé does not inherit an office merely through personal relationship with the former officeholder.

---

# Part VI — Handover and Institutional Continuity

## Article XVII — Officer Handover

### Section 1. Duty to Transfer

An outgoing officer shall transfer institutional materials reasonably necessary for the successor to perform the office.

### Section 2. Handover Materials

Depending upon the office, handover may include:

- records;
- account credentials;
- keys;
- devices;
- archives;
- financial documentation;
- calendars;
- contact information;
- pending matters;
- contracts;
- ceremonial property;
- and instructions concerning recurring responsibilities.

### Section 3. Institutional Accounts

Where practical, important digital services should use institution-controlled accounts rather than depending exclusively upon an officer's personal account.

### Section 4. Passwords and Credentials

Sensitive credentials shall be transferred securely.

Passwords should not be published in meeting minutes or ordinary public records.

### Section 5. Handover Record

For significant offices, completion of handover should be documented.

---

## Article XVIII — The Officer Binder

Every substantial office should maintain an **Officer Binder** or digital equivalent.

The Officer Binder should answer:

- What is this office responsible for?
- What happens regularly?
- What deadlines exist?
- Where are the records?
- What accounts or systems are used?
- Who needs to be contacted?
- What matters are currently pending?
- What decisions require approval from someone else?
- What should the next officer know that is not obvious from the governing documents?

The Binder should describe the office rather than the personality of its current holder.

An institution has achieved meaningful continuity when a competent successor can inherit an office without needing to reconstruct it from memory.

---

# Part VII — Training and Evaluation

## Article XIX — Training

### Section 1. Orientation

New officers should receive orientation appropriate to their responsibilities.

### Section 2. Common Training

Training may include:

- governing documents;
- ethical standards;
- recordkeeping;
- privacy;
- conflicts of interest;
- financial controls;
- ritual practice;
- safety;
- dispute procedures;
- and applicable legal responsibilities.

### Section 3. Proportionality

Training requirements should remain proportionate to the office.

A small volunteer Shrine should not construct unnecessary bureaucracy merely to imitate a large institution.

---

## Article XX — Evaluation

### Section 1. Purpose

Officer evaluation exists to improve institutional performance and identify problems before they become crises.

### Section 2. Review

Local Shrines and federated bodies may periodically review:

- whether responsibilities are being performed;
- whether authority remains appropriately distributed;
- whether records are current;
- whether conflicts require attention;
- and whether the office itself remains necessary.

### Section 3. Feedback

Reasonable mechanisms should exist for members or other officers to raise concerns concerning the performance of an office.

### Section 4. Office Redesign

Sometimes a problem lies not with the officer but with the design of the office.

Institutions should remain willing to clarify, divide, combine, or redesign responsibilities when experience demonstrates the need.

---

# Part VIII — Resignation, Removal, and Discipline

## Article XXI — Resignation

### Section 1. Right to Resign

An officer may resign.

Religious or administrative office is not a permanent personal obligation.

### Section 2. Notice

Where circumstances permit, reasonable notice should be provided to allow orderly succession.

### Section 3. Handover

Resignation does not eliminate the responsibility to return institutional property and facilitate reasonable transfer of records and access.

---

## Article XXII — Removal

### Section 1. Grounds

Grounds for removal may include:

- serious misconduct;
- abuse of authority;
- substantial violation of governing documents;
- financial misconduct;
- persistent failure to perform duties;
- incapacity;
- serious breach of confidentiality;
- retaliation;
- refusal to transfer institutional property;
- or other grounds established by applicable governing documents.

### Section 2. Procedure

Removal shall follow the procedure applicable to the office and Document VII — Dispute Procedure.

### Section 3. Notice and Response

Except where immediate temporary action is reasonably necessary for safety or preservation of institutional assets, an officer facing removal shall receive notice of the grounds and an opportunity to respond.

### Section 4. Interim Measures

An officer may be temporarily relieved of particular powers while a serious matter is reviewed.

Temporary restriction is not itself a final determination of misconduct.

### Section 5. Religious and Administrative Offices

Removal from an administrative office does not automatically remove a religious title unless the applicable standards require it.

Likewise, loss of a religious office does not automatically determine unrelated administrative positions.

---

# Part IX — Institutional Access and Property

## Article XXIII — Accounts and Digital Infrastructure

### Section 1. Institutional Control

Important institutional accounts should, where practical, remain recoverable by the institution rather than a single individual.

### Section 2. Access

Access shall be limited according to legitimate responsibility.

### Section 3. Departure

An outgoing officer shall not retain unauthorized control over institutional:

- email;
- websites;
- domains;
- social-media accounts;
- cloud storage;
- financial accounts;
- archives;
- or other digital infrastructure.

### Section 4. Recovery

Local Shrines should maintain reasonable recovery procedures for essential accounts.

---

## Article XXIV — Physical Property

### Section 1. Custody

An officer entrusted with institutional property acts as custodian rather than owner.

### Section 2. Inventory

Significant equipment, ceremonial objects, archives, keys, and other valuable property should be inventoried where practical.

### Section 3. Return

Institutional property shall be returned when the officer's responsibility for it ends.

---

# Part X — Interpretation

## Article XXV — Titles and Reality

### Section 1. Function Over Title

When uncertainty exists concerning an officer's authority, the governing documents and documented delegation control over assumptions based upon the title.

### Section 2. Themed Titles

Kitsune-Kyō may use imaginative, ceremonial, or theatrical titles.

Such titles do not eliminate the need to know:

- who may make a decision;
- who controls an account;
- who keeps a record;
- who may spend money;
- who may speak for the institution;
- and who is accountable when something goes wrong.

### Section 3. Mystery

Religious mystery is compatible with administrative clarity.

The Fox God may remain mysterious.

**The password recovery procedure should not.**

---

## Closing Principle

A healthy institution does not depend upon finding an irreplaceable person.

It creates roles that capable people can inhabit, responsibilities they can understand, records they can inherit, authority they can relinquish, and traditions they can pass forward.

The Metal Priestess tends the ritual.

The Kitsune Knight tends the gathering.

The Acolyte learns the way.

The administrator tends the machinery.

None possesses the Shrine.

All leave something usable for whoever comes next.

**Carry the flame. Pass the torch. Leave the passwords.**
