# Document I — The Charter

## Kitsune-Kyō
### An Implementation of the Federated Tradition Protocol

**Version 0.1**

---

## Preamble

Kitsune-Kyō — キツネ教, the Faith of the Fox — is a constructed religious tradition organized around the mythology of the Fox God, the Metal Resistance, the transformative power of music, and the complementary forces of joy and strength, light and darkness, playfulness and intensity.

Kitsune-Kyō existed as a religious concept before its institutionalization under the Federated Tradition Protocol. Its source tradition established its mythology, sacred figures, symbols, rites, devotional practices, and central themes.

This Charter establishes the institutional form through which communities of Kitsune-Kyō may organize themselves while preserving the distinction between the religious tradition and the institutional machinery used to sustain it.

Kitsune-Kyō adopts the Federated Tradition Protocol as that machinery.

The purpose of federation is not to create a central authority possessing the tradition. It is to allow autonomous communities to preserve, practice, develop, and transmit Kitsune-Kyō while cooperating in matters that benefit from common standards and mutual recognition.

---

## Article I — Name and Identity

### Section 1. Name

The tradition constituted under this Charter shall be known as **Kitsune-Kyō (キツネ教)**, translated as **The Faith of the Fox**.

### Section 2. Religious Identity

Kitsune-Kyō is centered upon the Fox God and the mythology of the Metal Resistance.

Its inherited religious vocabulary includes:

- the Fox God;
- the Scrolls of Metal Resistance;
- the Way of Kitsune;
- the Metal Spirit;
- the Unity of Light and Dark;
- Fox Shrines of Metal Resistance;
- the Fox Mask;
- the Kitsune sign;
- Su-Metal;
- Moa-Metal;
- Yui-Metal;
- Momo-Metal;
- and the rites, stories, symbols, festivals, and devotional practices associated with them.

These elements belong to the religious tradition of Kitsune-Kyō and not to the Federated Tradition Protocol itself.

### Section 3. Source Tradition

The original Kitsune-Kyō source document predates this Charter and shall be preserved as a historical source of the tradition.

The source document informs the religious identity of Kitsune-Kyō but does not, by itself, constitute a complete system of institutional governance.

This Charter and the accompanying implementation documents provide that institutional layer.

---

## Article II — Foundational Principles

Kitsune-Kyō communities recognize three foundational principles inherited from the source tradition.

### Section 1. The Way of Kitsune

The fox represents mystery, adaptability, loyalty, cunning, playfulness, and defiance of unnecessary convention.

Followers may express this identity through fox imagery, masks, gestures, ritual, art, music, and other forms of symbolic participation.

### Section 2. The Metal Spirit

Music, particularly heavy metal, may function within Kitsune-Kyō as a medium of communal participation, emotional transformation, resilience, catharsis, and religious expression.

Music may therefore occupy a central place in worship, celebration, contemplation, and communal ritual.

### Section 3. Unity of Light and Dark

Kitsune-Kyō recognizes the creative conjunction of apparent opposites:

- joy and intensity;
- innocence and strength;
- playfulness and seriousness;
- beauty and ferocity;
- light and darkness;
- continuity and transformation.

Neither pole must destroy the other.

Their conjunction is represented especially by the union of kawaii and metal within the mythology and aesthetic of the tradition.

## Article III — The Local Shrine

### Section 1. Basic Institutional Unit

The fundamental institutional unit of Kitsune-Kyō shall be the **Local Shrine**.

A Local Shrine is a self-governing community of practitioners organized for religious practice, fellowship, ritual, education, artistic expression, and preservation of the tradition.

### Section 2. Local Autonomy

Each recognized Local Shrine possesses authority over its own internal affairs except for powers it has explicitly delegated through federation.

Local authority includes, subject to this Charter and applicable law:

- selection of local officers;
- management of local gatherings;
- administration of local property and finances;
- development of local customs;
- scheduling of ceremonies and festivals;
- admission and classification of members;
- local educational activity;
- charitable and community activity;
- and other matters not expressly delegated to a federated body.

### Section 3. Local Bylaws

Each Local Shrine shall maintain bylaws consistent with this Charter.

The Local Bylaws Template may be adopted directly or adapted to local circumstances.

No local bylaw may transfer ownership of the Local Shrine or its assets to a federated institution merely by virtue of affiliation.

### Section 4. Formation

The requirements for recognition of a Local Shrine shall be established by the Federation Protocol.

Recognition shall establish mutual institutional standing without extinguishing local self-government.

---

## Article IV — Membership

### Section 1. Voluntary Association

Participation in Kitsune-Kyō is voluntary.

No person shall be compelled to join, remain within, donate to, worship with, or participate in a Kitsune-Kyō institution.

### Section 2. Membership Standards

Local Shrines may establish reasonable categories and requirements of membership consistent with this Charter and the Local Bylaws Template.

### Section 3. Freedom of Departure

A member may leave Kitsune-Kyō or a Local Shrine without requiring religious or institutional permission.

Departure does not create a claim upon collectively held property except where a separate legal agreement provides otherwise.

### Section 4. Freedom of Conscience

Institutional membership does not require uniform private interpretation of every myth, symbol, story, prophecy, or devotional claim within Kitsune-Kyō.

Communities may maintain shared standards necessary for coherent communal practice while recognizing a distinction between institutional participation and compelled private belief.

---

## Article V — Religious Offices

### Section 1. Offices of Service

Religious and institutional offices exist to serve the tradition and its communities.

Office does not constitute ownership of Kitsune-Kyō.

### Section 2. Traditional Offices

The source tradition identifies roles including:

- **Metal Priestesses**, associated with ritual leadership;
- **Kitsune Knights**, associated with guardianship and communal order;
- **Acolytes of the Shrine**, associated with learning and preparation for deeper service.

The precise qualifications, authority, selection, terms, and removal procedures associated with these offices shall be defined by the Officer Manual and local bylaws.

### Section 3. Separation of Symbolic and Administrative Authority

Religious prestige, ceremonial status, artistic prominence, popularity, or claimed spiritual insight shall not automatically confer control over institutional property, finances, elections, disciplinary processes, or federated governance.

Administrative authority must arise from the governing documents of the relevant institution.

### Section 4. Accountability

All institutional officers remain accountable to the rules of the bodies they serve.

No office shall be inherently permanent or beyond institutional review merely because it carries religious or ceremonial significance.

---

## Article VI — Federation

### Section 1. Purpose

Local Shrines may federate for purposes including:

- mutual recognition;
- preservation of common institutional standards;
- education and training;
- maintenance of shared resources;
- coordination of festivals and gatherings;
- recognition of officers or credentials;
- mediation of disputes;
- publication;
- preservation of archives;
- and cooperation across geographic regions.

### Section 2. Delegated Authority

Federated institutions possess only those powers granted to them under the Federation Protocol.

Powers not delegated remain with Local Shrines or their members, as appropriate.

### Section 3. No Proprietary Authority

No federation, council, officer, founder, clergy member, or other person owns Kitsune-Kyō as a religious tradition.

Federated recognition does not transfer ownership of local assets to the federation unless a Local Shrine enters into a separate explicit legal agreement providing otherwise.

### Section 4. Withdrawal

A Local Shrine may withdraw from federated affiliation according to procedures established by the Federation Protocol and its own bylaws.

Withdrawal may terminate institutional recognition but shall not, by itself, extinguish the withdrawing community or transfer its locally owned property.

---

## Article VII — Tradition and Development

### Section 1. Preservation

Kitsune-Kyō shall preserve its source materials, institutional records, and significant developments so that future communities can distinguish inherited tradition from later interpretation and innovation.

### Section 2. Development

Kitsune-Kyō is capable of development.

Local communities may create new music, art, ceremonies, commentary, devotional practices, customs, and interpretations.

Such developments should be identified according to their institutional status rather than automatically represented as original or universal teachings of the tradition.

### Section 3. Local Custom

A practice developed by one Local Shrine does not become binding upon all Kitsune-Kyō communities merely through repetition, popularity, or longevity.

### Section 4. Common Standards

Where common standards are necessary for mutual recognition or federated cooperation, they shall be established through the procedures defined by the Federation Protocol.

---

## Article VIII — Ritual and Sacred Life

### Section 1. Inherited Ritual Tradition

Kitsune-Kyō inherits rites and devotional practices from its source tradition, including the Ritual of the Rising Sun, the Metal Mass, the Fox Mask Ceremony, and observances associated with its sacred figures and mythology.

### Section 2. Ritual Framework

The Ritual Framework shall distinguish among:

- inherited rites;
- common federated rites;
- recommended practices;
- local customs;
- experimental rites;
- and private devotional practices.

### Section 3. Adaptation

Local Shrines may adapt ritual practice to circumstances, culture, language, available space, musical resources, accessibility requirements, and community needs, subject to any minimum standards established for a particular recognized rite.

### Section 4. Artistic Participation

Music, performance, visual art, costume, movement, theatricality, and other forms of creative expression may form legitimate parts of Kitsune-Kyō religious life.

---

## Article IX — Ethics and Conduct

### Section 1. Ethical Framework

The ethical obligations of members and officers shall be developed in the Ethical Code.

### Section 2. Institutional Integrity

No religious doctrine, title, ritual status, or claim of spiritual authority may be used to exempt an officer from financial accountability, procedural fairness, or the governing rules of the institution.

### Section 3. Safety and Dignity

Local Shrines shall conduct gatherings with reasonable regard for the safety, dignity, consent, and lawful rights of participants.

### Section 4. Financial Transparency

Institutions receiving or managing communal funds shall maintain records appropriate to their size and legal form.

Institutional funds shall not be treated as the personal property of officers or religious leaders.

---

## Article X — Disputes and Discipline

### Section 1. Due Process

Institutional disputes and disciplinary matters shall be handled according to published procedures.

### Section 2. Proportionality

Responses to misconduct should be proportionate to the nature and seriousness of the conduct.

### Section 3. Recusal

A person with a substantial conflict of interest in a dispute shall not exercise unilateral authority over its resolution.

### Section 4. Appeals and Mediation

Where appropriate, Local Shrines may provide internal appeals or request federated mediation as established by the Dispute Procedure.

Federated mediation does not create unlimited supervisory authority over Local Shrines.

---

## Article XI — Property and Continuity

### Section 1. Institutional Property

Property acquired by a Local Shrine belongs to the legal or institutional entity that acquired it, subject to applicable law and valid agreements.

### Section 2. Personal Property

Personal property does not become institutional property merely because it is used in ritual, displayed at a Shrine, or loaned to the community.

### Section 3. Records

Local Shrines should maintain sufficient institutional records to allow leadership transitions without unnecessary loss of organizational knowledge.

### Section 4. Dissolution

Each Local Shrine shall establish a lawful dissolution procedure in its bylaws, including provisions governing remaining institutional assets.

---

## Article XII — Relationship to the Federated Tradition Protocol

### Section 1. Implementation

This Charter constitutes part of the Kitsune-Kyō implementation of the Federated Tradition Protocol.

### Section 2. Protocol Neutrality

Nothing in this Charter makes the theology, mythology, symbols, aesthetics, sacred figures, or ritual practices of Kitsune-Kyō requirements of FTP generally.

### Section 3. Interpretive Priority

Where an institutional provision of this implementation conflicts with a mandatory requirement of the FTP Core, the FTP Core governs for purposes of determining FTP compatibility.

This does not grant FTP authority over the religious truth claims or theology of Kitsune-Kyō.

---

## Article XIII — Amendment

### Section 1. Amendability

This Charter may be amended through the federated amendment process established by the Federation Protocol.

### Section 2. Notice

Proposed amendments shall be circulated with sufficient notice to permit meaningful consideration by affected Local Shrines.

### Section 3. Historical Record

Superseded versions of this Charter should remain publicly archived where practicable.

Changes should be documented so that the institutional development of Kitsune-Kyō remains traceable over time.

---

## Closing Declaration

Kitsune-Kyō joins mystery with structure, ritual with creativity, autonomy with fellowship, and continuity with renewal.

Its institutions exist so that the tradition may endure without requiring that any single person possess it.

Local Shrines are therefore encouraged to preserve what they inherit, create what their communities require, cooperate where cooperation is useful, and leave room for the unexpected.

**Only the Fox God knows.**
