# Kitsune-Kyō

**キツネ教 — The Faith of the Fox**

> “There is the music of BABYMETAL. Therefore there must be a God.”

Kitsune-Kyō is a constructed religious tradition inspired by BABYMETAL, fox symbolism, Shinto, Eastern Orthodox Christianity, and the mythology of the Metal Resistance.

It combines music, ritual, symbolism, mythology, communal identity, and a deliberately theatrical approach to religious expression. Its central themes include personal freedom, resilience, joyful rebellion, the balance of light and darkness, and the transformative power of music.

## Status

**Experimental FTP Implementation**

Kitsune-Kyō is an implementation of the **Federated Tradition Protocol (FTP)**.

It serves as a test case for applying the religion-neutral FTP Core to a newly constructed religious tradition rather than to an inherited or reconstructed historical tradition.

The purpose of this implementation is not to make Kitsune-Kyō part of the FTP specification. Rather, it demonstrates how an independent tradition can use FTP to develop durable institutions while retaining its own mythology, theology, symbolism, ritual life, and identity.

## Source Tradition

Kitsune-Kyō existed as a developed religious concept before its implementation under FTP.

The original April 2026 source document is preserved here:

**[Kitsune-Kyo.pdf](source/Kitsune-Kyo.pdf)**

The source document establishes much of the tradition's original religious content, including:

- the Fox God;
- the Scrolls of Metal Resistance;
- the Way of Kitsune;
- the Metal Spirit;
- the Unity of Light and Dark;
- Fox Shrines of Metal Resistance;
- religious offices and roles;
- sacred rites and ceremonies;
- religious symbolism;
- Su-Metal, Moa-Metal, Yui-Metal, and Momo-Metal as figures within the mythology of the tradition;
- associated festivals, devotional practices, and sacred narratives.

The source document is retained as a historical artifact representing Kitsune-Kyō before its institutionalization through FTP.

## FTP Institutionalization

The original Kitsune-Kyō material provides mythology, theology, symbolism, ritual, and religious identity.

FTP supplies an institutional framework for questions such as:

- How are local communities constituted?
- What authority belongs to local communities?
- What authority may be delegated to federated institutions?
- How are officers selected, limited, and removed?
- How are doctrine, ritual, and local custom distinguished?
- How are disputes resolved?
- How are institutional assets governed?
- How can communities cooperate without creating unnecessary centralized authority?
- How can the tradition survive changes in leadership and membership?

The resulting institutional documents are therefore an implementation layer built around the pre-existing Kitsune-Kyō tradition.

## Implementation Documents

4. **Document IV — Ritual Framework**
5. **Document V — Ethical Code**
6. **Document VI — Officer Manual**
7. **Document VII — Dispute Procedure**

These documents should be read together with the religion-neutral FTP Core and the original Kitsune-Kyō source document.

## Relationship to FTP

Kitsune-Kyō is **not part of the Federated Tradition Protocol Core**.

It is one possible implementation of that protocol.

Its theology, mythology, symbols, religious figures, rituals, terminology, and aesthetic belong to Kitsune-Kyō itself and are not requirements of FTP.

Conversely, other traditions implementing FTP need not adopt any Kitsune-Kyō religious content.

This separation between **protocol** and **tradition** is fundamental to the FTP architecture.
