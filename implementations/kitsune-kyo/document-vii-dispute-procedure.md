# Document VII — Dispute Procedure

## Kitsune-Kyō
### An Implementation of the Federated Tradition Protocol

**Version 0.1**

---

## Preamble

Disagreement is inevitable wherever people share institutions.

A healthy tradition does not depend upon the absence of conflict. It depends upon possessing ways to address conflict without allowing every disagreement to become a struggle for control.

Kitsune-Kyō therefore distinguishes among:

- ordinary disagreement;
- interpersonal conflict;
- alleged misconduct;
- disputes concerning institutional authority;
- disputes between Local Shrines;
- and urgent matters involving safety or preservation of institutional assets.

This Procedure establishes processes for addressing such matters fairly, proportionately, and at the appropriate institutional level.

Its purpose is not to eliminate judgment.

Its purpose is to ensure that judgment occurs through procedures that can themselves be examined.

---

# Part I — General Principles

## Article I — Scope

### Section 1. Covered Matters

This Procedure may be used for disputes involving:

- interpretation of Local Shrine bylaws;
- interpretation of federated governing documents;
- membership decisions;
- officer conduct;
- alleged violations of the Ethical Code;
- use of institutional funds or property;
- access to institutional records or accounts;
- recognition of Local Shrines;
- disputes between Local Shrines;
- federated authority;
- and other institutional matters properly submitted under these governing documents.

### Section 2. Matters Outside Institutional Jurisdiction

Kitsune-Kyō institutions do not possess unlimited jurisdiction over the private lives of participants.

A disagreement does not become an institutional matter merely because the people involved belong to the same Shrine.

### Section 3. Legal Matters

Internal procedures do not replace applicable civil or criminal law.

Nothing in this Procedure requires a person to conceal conduct that may properly be reported to law enforcement, emergency services, regulators, insurers, professional authorities, or other lawful institutions.

### Section 4. Immediate Danger

Where immediate danger exists, protection of people takes priority over completion of ordinary internal procedures.

---

## Article II — Principles of Fair Procedure

### Section 1. Notice

A person facing material institutional action should receive reasonable notice of the issue.

### Section 2. Opportunity to Respond

A person whose conduct or institutional rights are materially at issue should ordinarily have an opportunity to respond.

### Section 3. Impartiality

A person with a substantial conflict of interest should not exercise unilateral authority over the resolution of the dispute.

### Section 4. Evidence

Decisions should be based upon information reasonably relevant to the matter rather than popularity, rumor, personal loyalty, or religious status.

### Section 5. Proportionality

Procedural complexity should correspond to the seriousness of the matter.

A minor misunderstanding should not require a tribunal.

A serious allegation should not be decided casually.

### Section 6. Timeliness

Disputes should be addressed within a reasonable time.

Delay should not become a method for avoiding accountability.

### Section 7. Documentation

Material decisions should be documented sufficiently to explain:

- what issue was considered;
- who possessed authority to decide it;
- what decision was reached;
- and what action, if any, follows.

---

# Part II — Levels of Resolution

## Article III — Informal Resolution

### Section 1. Ordinary Preference

Where appropriate, participants should attempt direct good-faith resolution before invoking formal institutional procedures.

### Section 2. Examples

Informal resolution may be appropriate for:

- misunderstandings;
- minor interpersonal friction;
- scheduling disputes;
- ordinary disagreements about local customs;
- accidental breaches of etiquette;
- and other matters unlikely to require formal institutional action.

### Section 3. No Requirement in Serious Cases

Direct confrontation is not required where a person reasonably believes it would be unsafe, coercive, futile, or inappropriate.

### Section 4. Documentation

Informal disagreements need not ordinarily become permanent institutional records unless they produce an agreement or reveal a matter requiring further action.

---

## Article IV — Facilitated Conversation

### Section 1. Purpose

Where direct discussion has failed or would benefit from assistance, the parties may seek a neutral facilitator.

### Section 2. Facilitator

The facilitator helps participants communicate.

The facilitator does not automatically possess authority to impose a decision.

### Section 3. Agreement

Where facilitated discussion produces an agreement, the parties may document it where useful.

### Section 4. Escalation

Failure of facilitated conversation does not itself establish that either party was wrong.

The matter may proceed to mediation or formal review where appropriate.

---

## Article V — Mediation

### Section 1. Purpose

Mediation seeks voluntary resolution with the assistance of a neutral mediator.

### Section 2. Voluntary Character

Except where governing documents require participation in an initial mediation process, no party shall be forced to accept a mediated settlement.

### Section 3. Mediator

A mediator should be reasonably impartial and should disclose substantial conflicts of interest.

### Section 4. Confidentiality

The participants should understand before mediation begins what degree of confidentiality applies and any limitations upon it.

### Section 5. Settlement

A mediated agreement should identify:

- the parties;
- the commitments made;
- any applicable deadlines;
- and whether the agreement is intended to resolve the matter finally.

### Section 6. Failure to Settle

Failure to reach settlement does not create a presumption against either party.

---

# Part III — Formal Complaints

## Article VI — Initiating a Formal Complaint

### Section 1. Complaint

A person or institution may initiate formal review by submitting a complaint to the body possessing jurisdiction.

### Section 2. Contents

Where practical, a complaint should identify:

- the complainant;
- the person or institution whose conduct is at issue;
- the conduct or decision being challenged;
- relevant dates;
- applicable governing provisions, if known;
- supporting information or records;
- and the remedy or institutional action requested, if any.

### Section 3. Substance Over Form

A complaint shall not be rejected merely because a participant fails to use technical terminology or identify the exact governing provision.

### Section 4. Good Faith

Knowingly fabricating material allegations may itself constitute misconduct.

An allegation that is not ultimately substantiated is not, merely for that reason, a false or bad-faith complaint.

---

## Article VII — Initial Review

### Section 1. Jurisdiction

The receiving body shall first determine whether the matter falls within its institutional authority.

### Section 2. Sufficiency

The reviewer may determine whether the complaint contains enough information to identify an issue appropriate for review.

### Section 3. Clarification

Reasonable clarification or additional information may be requested.

### Section 4. Alternative Resolution

A matter may be referred to informal resolution or mediation where appropriate.

### Section 5. Dismissal

A complaint may be dismissed where:

- the institution lacks jurisdiction;
- the allegations, even if true, would not create an institutional issue;
- the matter has already received final resolution and no legitimate basis for reopening exists;
- or the complaint is so deficient that meaningful review is impossible after reasonable opportunity for clarification.

### Section 6. Record

A material dismissal should be documented with a brief explanation.

---

# Part IV — Notice and Response

## Article VIII — Notice to the Respondent

### Section 1. Notice

When formal review proceeds, the respondent shall ordinarily receive notice of the material allegations.

### Section 2. Information

Notice should provide enough information for a meaningful response.

### Section 3. Evidence

Relevant supporting material should be made available where appropriate, subject to legitimate privacy, safety, legal, or confidentiality concerns.

### Section 4. Time to Respond

The respondent shall receive a reasonable period to respond.

### Section 5. No Presumption from Silence

Failure to respond may permit the process to continue but shall not automatically be treated as a confession.

---

## Article IX — Response

### Section 1. Contents

A respondent may provide:

- an explanation;
- admissions or denials;
- relevant documents;
- witness information;
- contextual information;
- procedural objections;
- and proposed resolution.

### Section 2. Counter-Allegations

A response containing materially distinct allegations may be treated as a separate complaint where necessary for fair consideration.

### Section 3. Cooperation

Participants should preserve relevant institutional records and cooperate reasonably with legitimate review procedures.

---

# Part V — Fact-Finding

## Article X — Review of Information

### Section 1. Relevant Information

The decision-maker may consider reasonably relevant:

- documents;
- messages;
- financial records;
- meeting records;
- photographs;
- recordings;
- witness accounts;
- institutional logs;
- physical evidence;
- and other reliable information.

### Section 2. Reliability

Not all information deserves equal weight.

Decision-makers should consider:

- firsthand versus secondhand knowledge;
- contemporaneous records;
- consistency;
- corroboration;
- possible bias;
- completeness;
- and other circumstances affecting reliability.

### Section 3. Rumor

Rumor may identify a matter requiring inquiry but should not ordinarily serve as the sole basis for serious institutional sanctions.

### Section 4. Missing Evidence

The absence of a record should not automatically prove that an event did or did not occur.

Its significance depends upon whether such a record would ordinarily be expected to exist.

### Section 5. Digital Material

Digital evidence should be evaluated with reasonable attention to authenticity, context, completeness, and possible alteration.

---

## Article XI — Standard of Decision

### Section 1. Ordinary Institutional Matters

Unless another standard is expressly required, formal internal decisions should be based upon the decision-maker's reasoned judgment after considering the available information.

### Section 2. Serious Consequences

The more serious and irreversible the proposed institutional consequence, the stronger the evidentiary basis should ordinarily be.

### Section 3. Uncertainty

A decision may acknowledge uncertainty.

Institutional procedures do not require false certainty merely to produce closure.

### Section 4. Insufficient Evidence

A finding that evidence is insufficient does not necessarily establish that the underlying allegation was false.

---

# Part VI — Decision-Makers

## Article XII — Local Shrine Matters

### Section 1. Ordinary Jurisdiction

Local disputes should ordinarily be resolved by the Local Shrine possessing jurisdiction.

### Section 2. Decision-Making Body

The applicable bylaws may assign formal decisions to:

- an officer;
- the Local Council;
- a disciplinary panel;
- the voting membership;
- or another defined body.

### Section 3. Conflicts

Where the normal decision-maker has a substantial conflict of interest, an alternate procedure shall be used.

### Section 4. Small Shrines

A small Local Shrine may request assistance from another recognized Shrine or the federation where it lacks enough disinterested persons to conduct a fair process.

---

## Article XIII — Federated Matters

### Section 1. Federation Jurisdiction

Federated procedures may address:

- disputes between Local Shrines;
- recognition status;
- conduct of federated officers;
- interpretation of federated standards;
- misuse of federated credentials or resources;
- and other matters within delegated federation authority.

### Section 2. Council of Shrines

The Council of Shrines possesses ultimate ordinary authority over federated institutional matters except where another procedure has been established by the governing documents.

### Section 3. Panels

The Council may establish neutral panels to conduct fact-finding, hearings, mediation, or recommendations.

### Section 4. Limits

Federated dispute jurisdiction shall not be used as a pretext for taking control of matters reserved to Local Shrines.

---

# Part VII — Conflicts and Recusal

## Article XIV — Conflicts of Interest

### Section 1. Disclosure

A decision-maker shall disclose a substantial relationship or interest reasonably relevant to impartiality.

### Section 2. Examples

Potential conflicts may include:

- being a party to the dispute;
- close family relationship;
- romantic relationship;
- significant financial interest;
- substantial personal hostility;
- direct involvement in the disputed conduct;
- or another circumstance creating a serious question of impartiality.

### Section 3. Recusal

Where a conflict is substantial, the person should recuse from adjudication.

### Section 4. Institutional Knowledge

Prior knowledge of a matter does not automatically require recusal.

The question is whether the person can fairly exercise the assigned role.

---

# Part VIII — Interim Measures

## Article XV — Temporary Action

### Section 1. Purpose

Temporary measures may be used where reasonably necessary to:

- protect participants;
- preserve evidence;
- protect institutional funds or property;
- secure digital accounts;
- prevent retaliation;
- maintain essential operations;
- or avoid serious disruption while review occurs.

### Section 2. Examples

Interim measures may include:

- temporary suspension from an office;
- temporary restriction of account access;
- temporary reassignment of responsibilities;
- no-contact expectations;
- temporary event restrictions;
- preservation of records;
- or other proportionate measures.

### Section 3. Not a Final Finding

An interim measure is not itself a determination that misconduct occurred.

### Section 4. Review

Temporary measures should be reviewed periodically and should not continue longer than reasonably necessary without justification.

---

# Part IX — Hearings

## Article XVI — Formal Hearing

### Section 1. When Appropriate

A formal hearing may be used where:

- facts are materially disputed;
- serious sanctions are possible;
- institutional interpretation requires structured argument;
- or the decision-making body determines that a hearing would materially improve fairness.

### Section 2. Format

A hearing may occur:

- in person;
- remotely;
- through written submissions;
- or through a combination of methods.

### Section 3. Participation

The parties should receive a reasonable opportunity to present relevant information and respond to material claims.

### Section 4. Order

The presiding person may establish reasonable procedures to keep the hearing focused, respectful, and manageable.

### Section 5. Record

A material formal hearing should produce an adequate institutional record.

---

# Part X — Decisions and Remedies

## Article XVII — Written Decision

### Section 1. Decision

A formal decision should ordinarily identify:

- the issue;
- the decision-maker;
- material findings;
- the governing rule or authority;
- the result;
- any remedy or sanction;
- and any available appeal.

### Section 2. Privacy

A decision need not disclose confidential information beyond what is reasonably necessary.

### Section 3. Distribution

The decision shall be provided to persons or institutions entitled to receive it.

---

## Article XVIII — Remedies

### Section 1. Range of Responses

Institutional responses may include:

- no action;
- clarification;
- correction of a record;
- apology;
- mediated agreement;
- training;
- warning;
- restitution where appropriate;
- restriction of particular authority;
- reassignment;
- temporary suspension;
- removal from office;
- membership restriction;
- termination of membership;
- suspension of Shrine recognition;
- loss of Shrine recognition;
- or another lawful and proportionate remedy.

### Section 2. Tailoring

A remedy should address the institutional problem actually established.

### Section 3. Multiple Measures

More than one measure may be appropriate.

### Section 4. Restoration

A decision may establish conditions under which restrictions may later be reconsidered.

### Section 5. Authority

No decision-maker may impose a remedy outside the authority granted to that decision-maker.

---

# Part XI — Appeals and Review

## Article XIX — Appeal

### Section 1. Availability

An appeal may be available where provided by Local Shrine bylaws, federated procedures, or this document.

### Section 2. Grounds

Grounds for appeal may include:

- substantial procedural error;
- significant new evidence not reasonably available earlier;
- material conflict of interest;
- a decision outside institutional authority;
- substantial misapplication of governing documents;
- or a remedy grossly disproportionate to the findings.

### Section 3. Disagreement Alone

An appeal is not automatically warranted merely because a party dislikes the result.

### Section 4. Appellate Body

An appeal should be heard by a person or body not responsible for the original final decision where reasonably possible.

### Section 5. Possible Results

An appellate body may:

- affirm;
- modify;
- reverse;
- remand for further proceedings;
- or determine that it lacks jurisdiction.

---

## Article XX — Finality and Reopening

### Section 1. Finality

Institutions benefit from decisions eventually becoming final.

### Section 2. Reopening

A final matter may be reopened where substantial new evidence, fraud, serious procedural defect, or another compelling institutional reason justifies reconsideration.

### Section 3. Repeated Complaints

A dispute process should not be used to harass another person through endless resubmission of substantially identical rejected claims.

---

# Part XII — Retaliation and Abuse of Process

## Article XXI — Retaliation

### Section 1. Protection

Institutional retaliation against a person merely for good-faith participation in a complaint, investigation, mediation, hearing, or appeal is prohibited.

### Section 2. Examples

Retaliation may include improper:

- removal from activities;
- threats;
- harassment;
- disclosure of private information;
- manipulation of office;
- financial punishment;
- or social pressure exercised through institutional authority.

### Section 3. Independent Misconduct

Participation in a dispute does not immunize a person from consequences for separate misconduct.

---

## Article XXII — Abuse of Process

### Section 1. Bad Faith

Dispute procedures shall not knowingly be used to:

- harass;
- intimidate;
- silence legitimate criticism;
- manufacture institutional leverage;
- pursue knowingly fabricated allegations;
- or repeatedly relitigate matters without legitimate basis.

### Section 2. Care in Application

A finding against a complainant does not by itself establish abuse of process.

Bad faith should not be inferred merely because an allegation could not be proven.

---

# Part XIII — Inter-Shrine Disputes

## Article XXIII — Disputes Between Local Shrines

### Section 1. Direct Communication

Local Shrines should ordinarily attempt direct institutional communication before invoking formal federation procedures.

### Section 2. Mediation

The federation may offer mediation.

### Section 3. Formal Federation Review

Formal review may occur where the dispute concerns:

- federated recognition;
- common standards;
- shared resources;
- agreements between Shrines;
- representation of federated authority;
- or another matter within federation jurisdiction.

### Section 4. Local Autonomy

The federation shall not resolve an inter-shrine dispute by simply assuming general control over either Shrine.

### Section 5. Separation

Where cooperation has become impractical, orderly disengagement may be an appropriate resolution.

Not every dispute requires reconciliation.

---

# Part XIV — Recognition Proceedings

## Article XXIV — Local Shrine Recognition

### Section 1. Review

A question concerning suspension or loss of federated recognition shall proceed with notice and an opportunity for the affected Shrine to respond.

### Section 2. Representation

The Local Shrine may designate one or more persons to represent it during the proceeding.

### Section 3. Findings

A decision affecting recognition should identify the institutional requirements at issue and the basis for the decision.

### Section 4. Corrective Opportunity

Where reasonable, a Shrine should receive an opportunity to correct remediable deficiencies before permanent loss of recognition.

### Section 5. Emergency Suspension

Temporary suspension may occur before completion of ordinary proceedings where immediate action is reasonably necessary to protect the federation, its participants, assets, or integrity.

Such suspension should receive prompt subsequent review.

---

# Part XV — Records and Privacy

## Article XXV — Dispute Records

### Section 1. Preservation

Material formal dispute records shall be preserved according to applicable institutional recordkeeping policies.

### Section 2. Access

Access should be limited according to legitimate institutional need, participant rights, privacy, and applicable law.

### Section 3. Public Statements

Institutions should exercise care when publicly discussing disputes involving identifiable individuals.

### Section 4. Institutional History

Where a dispute produces a significant interpretation of governing documents, the resulting institutional principle may be preserved without unnecessarily publishing private details.

---

# Part XVI — Relationship with External Authorities

## Article XXVI — Civil and Criminal Matters

### Section 1. No Substitute for Law

Kitsune-Kyō dispute procedures are internal institutional mechanisms.

They do not constitute courts of law.

### Section 2. Reporting

Nothing in this Procedure prohibits a participant from making a lawful report to an appropriate external authority.

### Section 3. Emergency Services

Internal processes should not delay appropriate emergency assistance.

### Section 4. Legal Obligations

Institutions and officers shall comply with applicable legal duties concerning reporting, preservation of evidence, subpoenas, court orders, and other lawful requirements.

### Section 5. Internal Continuation

Where appropriate and lawful, an internal institutional process may proceed independently of an external proceeding.

Care should be taken not to interfere with lawful investigations.

---

# Part XVII — Interpretation and Development

## Article XXVII — Procedural Flexibility

### Section 1. Proportionality

This Procedure establishes principles and default mechanisms rather than requiring identical proceedings for every dispute.

### Section 2. Small Communities

A small Shrine may use simpler procedures where they remain fundamentally fair.

### Section 3. Serious Matters

Greater formality may be appropriate where consequences are severe, facts are complex, or institutional interests are substantial.

### Section 4. Accessibility

Procedures should make reasonable accommodations for participants who require them to participate meaningfully.

---

## Article XXVIII — Precedent

### Section 1. Prior Decisions

Previous decisions may provide useful guidance.

### Section 2. Non-Absolute Effect

A prior institutional decision does not automatically control every later case involving superficially similar facts.

### Section 3. Consistency

Material departures from established interpretations should be explainable.

### Section 4. Development

Accumulated decisions may help Kitsune-Kyō institutions clarify ambiguous provisions and improve future revisions of the governing documents.

---

## Article XXIX — Amendment

This Procedure may be amended according to the amendment mechanisms established by the Kitsune-Kyō Charter and Federation Protocol.

Significant revisions should preserve prior versions where practical so that institutional development remains traceable.

---

# Closing Principle

Conflict does not mean that an institution has failed.

Sometimes conflict reveals precisely where an institution needs structure.

Kitsune-Kyō therefore seeks neither endless harmony nor endless judgment.

It seeks procedures capable of distinguishing disagreement from misconduct, accusation from evidence, authority from personality, temporary precaution from final judgment, and accountability from revenge.

Some disagreements can be resolved.

Some can be mediated.

Some require decisions.

Some require separation.

The institution should know which is which.

**Only the Fox God knows everything.**

**Everyone else needs evidence and due process.**
