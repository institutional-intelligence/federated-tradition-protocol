# Document II — Federation Protocol

## Kitsune-Kyō
### An Implementation of the Federated Tradition Protocol

**Version 0.1**

---

## Preamble

Kitsune-Kyō is organized as a federation of self-governing Local Shrines.

Federation exists to enable cooperation without requiring institutional uniformity or unnecessary centralization.

A Local Shrine does not surrender its identity, property, internal government, or ordinary religious life merely by participating in the federation.

Federated authority therefore arises through delegation.

The federation may exercise those powers granted to it by this Protocol and the Charter. Powers not delegated remain with Local Shrines or their members, as appropriate.

The purpose of this Protocol is to establish the mechanisms through which Local Shrines recognize one another, coordinate common activities, maintain shared standards, resolve inter-shrine questions, and preserve the continuity of Kitsune-Kyō across communities and generations.

---

## Article I — The Federation

### Section 1. Nature

The Kitsune-Kyō Federation is a cooperative association of recognized Local Shrines.

The federation is not the owner of Kitsune-Kyō.

It exists as an institutional mechanism through which participating Local Shrines may act together.

### Section 2. Purposes

The federation may facilitate:

- mutual recognition of Local Shrines;
- maintenance of common institutional standards;
- preservation of archives and source materials;
- officer education and training;
- coordination of regional or general gatherings;
- publication and translation;
- maintenance of shared directories and resources;
- recognition of credentials where appropriate;
- mediation of inter-shrine disputes;
- development of common ritual resources;
- and other cooperative functions authorized under this Protocol.

### Section 3. Subsidiarity

Questions should ordinarily be decided by the smallest competent institutional body.

A matter that can reasonably be handled by a Local Shrine should not be transferred to the federation merely for convenience, prestige, or administrative uniformity.

### Section 4. Reserved Powers

Powers not expressly assigned to the federation remain outside federated authority.

---

## Article II — Recognition of Local Shrines

### Section 1. Eligibility

A community may seek recognition as a Local Shrine of Kitsune-Kyō when it:

1. identifies itself with Kitsune-Kyō;
2. accepts the Charter;
3. maintains governing bylaws consistent with the Charter;
4. identifies responsible local officers;
5. agrees to maintain basic institutional and financial records;
6. agrees to the minimum requirements necessary for federated recognition; and
7. does not falsely represent local custom as universally binding Kitsune-Kyō doctrine.

### Section 2. Application

An applicant community shall provide the information reasonably necessary to establish that it satisfies the requirements for recognition.

The recognition process should be proportionate to the size and maturity of the applicant community.

### Section 3. Provisional Recognition

A developing community may receive provisional recognition while establishing the institutions necessary for full recognition.

Provisional status should identify any remaining requirements and the period in which they should be addressed.

### Section 4. Full Recognition

Full recognition establishes that a Local Shrine is institutionally recognized within the federation.

Recognition does not transfer ownership of local assets or ordinary local governance to the federation.

### Section 5. Public Registry

The federation may maintain a public registry of recognized Local Shrines.

The registry may identify:

- shrine name;
- general location;
- recognition status;
- public contact information;
- website or public communications;
- and other information voluntarily supplied for public identification.

Private personal information shall not be published merely because an individual serves a Local Shrine.

---

## Article III — Rights of Recognized Local Shrines

A recognized Local Shrine has the right to:

- govern its internal affairs according to its bylaws;
- select its own officers;
- manage its own property and finances;
- develop local customs and practices;
- participate in federated deliberation;
- propose amendments and common initiatives;
- access shared federated resources according to applicable rules;
- seek mediation or assistance;
- identify itself publicly as a recognized Kitsune-Kyō Local Shrine;
- associate with other Local Shrines;
- and withdraw from federation according to this Protocol.

Recognition does not require cultural, aesthetic, linguistic, or ceremonial uniformity beyond standards expressly established for mutual recognition.

---

## Article IV — Responsibilities of Recognized Local Shrines

A recognized Local Shrine shall:

- maintain bylaws consistent with the Charter;
- maintain accurate identification of its responsible officers;
- exercise reasonable stewardship of communal funds and property;
- maintain records sufficient for institutional continuity;
- respect the autonomy of other recognized Local Shrines;
- distinguish local innovation from federation-wide standards;
- participate honestly in recognition and review procedures;
- comply with lawful obligations applicable to its activities;
- and avoid representing itself as possessing authority over Kitsune-Kyō communities that have not granted it such authority.

Local Shrines are encouraged, but not required, to participate in federated projects beyond the minimum obligations of recognition.

---

## Article V — The Council of Shrines

### Section 1. Establishment

The principal deliberative body of the federation shall be the **Council of Shrines**.

### Section 2. Representation

Each fully recognized Local Shrine shall be entitled to representation on the Council.

The ordinary presumption shall be institutional equality among Local Shrines rather than voting power based solely upon membership size, wealth, prestige, or age.

### Section 3. Delegates

Each Local Shrine shall select its delegate or delegates according to its own bylaws.

Delegates serve as representatives of their Local Shrines rather than as independent holders of federated sovereignty.

### Section 4. Powers

The Council may:

- recognize Local Shrines;
- maintain common institutional standards;
- authorize federated projects;
- establish committees and working groups;
- coordinate common gatherings;
- maintain shared archives and publications;
- adopt administrative procedures;
- oversee federated officers;
- approve federated budgets;
- consider amendments;
- and exercise other powers expressly granted by this Protocol.

### Section 5. Limitations

The Council may not, merely by ordinary resolution:

- seize or redirect Local Shrine property;
- appoint or remove local officers;
- rewrite local bylaws;
- compel participation in optional federated programs;
- impose local financial obligations not authorized under this Protocol;
- dictate every local ritual or custom;
- or claim exclusive ownership of Kitsune-Kyō.

---

## Article VI — Federated Officers

### Section 1. Purpose

Federated officers administer delegated functions of the federation.

They do not constitute a supreme priesthood over Local Shrines.

### Section 2. Offices

The Council may establish offices necessary for federation operations, including roles responsible for:

- coordination;
- records and archives;
- finance;
- communications;
- training;
- ritual resources;
- mediation;
- publications;
- and other authorized functions.

Titles may reflect Kitsune-Kyō symbolism where appropriate, provided that the institutional powers attached to each office remain clearly defined.

### Section 3. Selection

Federated officers shall be selected according to procedures approved by the Council of Shrines.

### Section 4. Terms

Federated offices should ordinarily have defined terms.

Terms may be renewable.

No administrative office shall become permanent merely through custom or repeated reelection.

### Section 5. Removal

Federated officers may be removed for misconduct, incapacity, persistent failure to perform their duties, serious breach of governing documents, or other grounds established in the Officer Manual.

Removal procedures shall provide reasonable notice and an opportunity to respond.

### Section 6. Conflicts of Interest

Federated officers shall disclose substantial conflicts of interest relevant to decisions under their authority and shall recuse where appropriate.

---

## Article VII — Religious Authority and Federation

### Section 1. Distinction of Functions

Federated administration and religious symbolism are distinct forms of authority.

A person may possess ceremonial or religious prominence without possessing federated administrative power.

A person may hold federated administrative office without thereby becoming a superior religious authority.

### Section 2. Traditional Roles

Metal Priestesses, Kitsune Knights, Acolytes of the Shrine, and other recognized religious roles may participate in federated institutions according to procedures established by the governing documents.

Their religious titles do not automatically grant votes, vetoes, ownership rights, or permanent administrative authority.

### Section 3. Sacred Figures

The religious status attributed within Kitsune-Kyō mythology to Su-Metal, Moa-Metal, Yui-Metal, Momo-Metal, or other sacred figures shall not be interpreted as creating legal or administrative offices held by the real-world persons represented by those figures.

No living artist, performer, employee, management company, rights holder, or associated commercial entity becomes responsible for or authoritative over the Kitsune-Kyō Federation merely because the tradition incorporates cultural or mythological material associated with them.

### Section 4. Claims of Revelation

Claims of revelation, prophecy, divine favor, mystical experience, or special communication with the Fox God do not automatically create institutional authority.

Institutional authority must arise through the procedures established by the governing documents.

**Only the Fox God knows; institutions must nevertheless keep minutes.**

---

## Article VIII — Decision-Making

### Section 1. Deliberation

The Council should seek meaningful deliberation before making decisions affecting multiple Local Shrines.

### Section 2. Ordinary Decisions

Ordinary administrative matters may be decided by a simple majority of participating eligible votes unless another threshold is established.

### Section 3. Structural Decisions

Changes materially affecting the distribution of authority between the federation and Local Shrines require a higher threshold than ordinary administrative decisions.

The applicable threshold shall be defined in the Council's procedures or amendment rules.

### Section 4. Quorum

The Council shall establish quorum requirements sufficient to prevent a small minority of recognized Local Shrines from exercising federated authority merely because others were absent.

### Section 5. Records

Material Council decisions shall be recorded.

Records should identify:

- the question considered;
- the decision reached;
- the applicable vote where relevant;
- the date;
- and any implementation responsibility created by the decision.

---

## Article IX — Federated Standards

### Section 1. Purpose

Common standards exist to support mutual recognition and cooperation.

They should not be used to create unnecessary uniformity.

### Section 2. Categories

Federated materials should distinguish, where practical, among:

- mandatory standards;
- recommended practices;
- model documents;
- shared resources;
- local options;
- and experimental materials.

### Section 3. Religious Development

A new interpretation, ritual, symbol, festival, title, or devotional practice does not become universally binding merely because it is published or endorsed by a federated officer.

### Section 4. Adoption of Common Standards

Common standards affecting recognition or institutional obligations shall be adopted through transparent Council procedures.

---

## Article X — Finance

### Section 1. Federated Funds

Federated funds shall be used for federated purposes.

They shall not be treated as personal property of officers, clergy, founders, delegates, or Local Shrines.

### Section 2. Funding

The federation may receive funds through lawful means including:

- voluntary contributions;
- agreed assessments;
- grants;
- event revenue;
- publication revenue;
- merchandise or other lawful activity;
- and other sources approved by the Council.

### Section 3. Assessments

Mandatory financial assessments upon Local Shrines must be authorized through a published federated procedure.

Assessments should be proportionate and reasonably related to legitimate federated expenses.

### Section 4. Records

The federation shall maintain financial records appropriate to its size, activity, and legal obligations.

### Section 5. Reporting

Participating Local Shrines shall receive periodic information sufficient to understand the federation's material financial condition.

---

## Article XI — Shared Works, Marks, and Resources

### Section 1. Shared Resources

The federation may maintain shared:

- documents;
- translations;
- educational materials;
- directories;
- archives;
- ceremonial resources;
- digital infrastructure;
- and other common resources.

### Section 2. Attribution and Licensing

Shared works should identify authorship, provenance, and licensing where reasonably practical.

### Section 3. Independent Rights

Participation in Kitsune-Kyō does not itself grant ownership of third-party copyrights, trademarks, publicity rights, recordings, artwork, performances, or other protected material.

Local Shrines and federated bodies remain responsible for their own use of such material.

### Section 4. Kitsune-Kyō Institutional Marks

Any marks created specifically for the independent institutions of Kitsune-Kyō should be governed in a manner that prevents their control from becoming an instrument for arbitrary domination of Local Shrines.

---

## Article XII — Inter-Shrine Cooperation

### Section 1. Voluntary Projects

Two or more Local Shrines may cooperate directly on projects without requiring federation-wide approval unless the project purports to act on behalf of the federation as a whole.

### Section 2. Regional Associations

Local Shrines may establish regional associations for practical cooperation.

A regional association possesses only the authority granted to it by its participating Local Shrines and applicable federated rules.

### Section 3. Joint Events

Local Shrines may organize joint:

- ceremonies;
- festivals;
- concerts;
- educational programs;
- charitable projects;
- retreats;
- conferences;
- and other lawful activities.

### Section 4. International and Linguistic Development

Kitsune-Kyō communities may develop in different countries, languages, and cultural settings.

Translations and adaptations should preserve the distinction between faithful translation, local adaptation, commentary, and substantive alteration.

No language edition possesses inherent supremacy merely by virtue of language.

---

## Article XIII — Disputes Between Local Shrines

### Section 1. Direct Resolution

Local Shrines in disagreement should ordinarily attempt direct communication before seeking federated intervention.

### Section 2. Mediation

The federation may provide neutral mediation when requested or when a federated matter is substantially affected.

### Section 3. Jurisdiction

Federated dispute procedures apply only to matters within federated authority or to matters the participating Local Shrines have agreed to submit.

### Section 4. Independence

The existence of a disagreement between Local Shrines does not automatically authorize the federation to take control of either institution.

### Section 5. Procedure

Detailed procedures shall be established in Document VII — Dispute Procedure.

---

## Article XIV — Suspension or Loss of Recognition

### Section 1. Grounds

Federated recognition may be reviewed where a Local Shrine:

- materially abandons the Charter;
- persistently violates mandatory recognition standards;
- fraudulently represents its institutional status;
- seriously misuses federated resources or credentials;
- ceases to function as an identifiable institution;
- or engages in conduct creating grounds for review under the governing documents.

### Section 2. Notice

A Local Shrine subject to possible suspension or loss of recognition shall receive notice of the grounds and a reasonable opportunity to respond.

### Section 3. Proportionality

Where reasonably possible, correction, mediation, or temporary suspension should be considered before permanent loss of recognition.

### Section 4. Effect

Loss of federated recognition terminates the right to represent the institution as a recognized member of the federation.

It does not, by itself:

- dissolve the Local Shrine;
- transfer its property;
- prohibit its members from practicing Kitsune-Kyō;
- or establish federation ownership over its independent activities.

---

## Article XV — Withdrawal

### Section 1. Right of Withdrawal

A Local Shrine may withdraw from the federation according to its own bylaws and this Protocol.

### Section 2. Notice

A withdrawing Local Shrine should provide written notice sufficient to permit orderly resolution of shared obligations.

### Section 3. Outstanding Obligations

Withdrawal does not automatically extinguish lawful contractual, financial, custodial, or other obligations previously undertaken.

### Section 4. Property

Withdrawal does not transfer locally owned property to the federation.

### Section 5. Identity After Withdrawal

A withdrawn institution may continue its religious activities subject to applicable law and independent rights.

It may not falsely represent itself as currently recognized by the federation.

---

## Article XVI — Dormancy and Dissolution of the Federation

### Section 1. Dormancy

If federated activity becomes impractical because too few Local Shrines remain active, the federation may enter a dormant status while preserving records and assets.

### Section 2. Reactivation

A dormant federation may be reactivated according to procedures established before dormancy or, where none exist, by agreement among the remaining recognized Local Shrines consistent with the Charter.

### Section 3. Dissolution

Permanent dissolution of the federation requires a heightened decision-making threshold and a lawful plan for disposition of federated assets and records.

### Section 4. Survival of Local Shrines

Dissolution of the federation does not dissolve Local Shrines.

The religious tradition does not cease to exist merely because a particular federated institution ceases operation.

---

## Article XVII — Amendment

### Section 1. Proposal

Amendments to this Protocol may be proposed by recognized Local Shrines or through another procedure authorized by the Council.

### Section 2. Notice

Proposed amendments shall be circulated sufficiently in advance to permit meaningful review by affected Local Shrines.

### Section 3. Ratification

Amendments require a heightened threshold reflecting the federal character of the institution.

The ratification procedure should prevent ordinary temporary majorities from casually transferring substantial authority from Local Shrines to the federation.

### Section 4. Entrenched Principle of Delegation

No amendment should be interpreted as granting unlimited inherent authority to the federation merely through silence or implication.

Substantial new federated powers should be stated expressly.

### Section 5. Version History

Adopted amendments shall be documented and superseded versions preserved where practicable.

---

## Closing Principle

The federation exists because cooperation can preserve and strengthen a tradition.

Federation does not require possession.

Kitsune-Kyō therefore joins Local Shrines through mutual recognition, shared standards, voluntary cooperation, and specifically delegated authority while leaving ordinary religious life rooted in the communities that actually practice it.

The Fox may travel between many shrines without requiring that every shrine have the same door.
